1. Summary

Stub. A second visitor's photo waits in a queue at the front instead of getting a 503, and the page shows their place and an estimated time for their answer. The inference host keeps one worker. Whether photos from several visitors can be batched through the models is researched, not assumed. Nothing is designed yet.

2. Background

Read at 6c45b78, updated at d9212c6; to be checked when the ticket is written.

  • One worker, one request. gunicorn runs one sync worker with --timeout 30 (webapp/deploy/coffee-cv-web.service.template). nginx caps the backend at one request in flight server-wide (limit_conn 1) and gives a second request an immediate 503 (webapp/deploy/coffee-cv.nginx.conf.template). After OPS-7 the page shows a busy message for it (OPS-7 D12). After OPS-15 that gate is at the front, with a backstop on the inference host (OPS-15 D11).
  • How long the worker is busy. After OPS-7, every pick costs a full classification in one request: about 9.4 s median and 11.4 s max server-side on yappy's GPU (ML-7 P10, 13 fixtures, /classify, segmenter included). Before OPS-7, a visitor who stopped at the mask held it for about 2 s (OPS-7 R6).
  • The crossing. After OPS-15 a photo first crosses from the front to yappy at about 2.1 Mbit/s: up to about 20 s for the 5 MB cap (OPS-15 D4, D15). yappy's nginx buffers the whole upload before the worker sees it (OPS-15 D10), so one visitor's crossing could overlap another's inference. An estimate of the wait has to count both.
  • The front's budget. About 200 MB of the proxy's memory available (OPS-15 §2): a queue there holds at most a few 5 MB photos.
  • The models are loaded per worker. Each worker loads and warms every graph at boot (ADR 0021), and on the GPU it must prove the GPU ran the warm-up (ADR 0020). A second worker would not fit beside the first (ADR 0026), and the owner rules it out (2026-11-02).
  • Batching. A photo is 40 patches through DINOv3, run at the batch its LiteRT manifest names. The converted graphs are built for that batch (ADR 0022), so batching patches from several photos means fitting them to that batch or converting again. The segmenter runs one photo at a time.
  • Constraints. No state between requests (ADR 0023): a queued photo waits inside its own open request; no containers on the server (.out-of-scope/containerised-serving.md); the request log records latency_ms and preview_ms, which show how busy the worker is.

3. Questions to research

  • How the front holds a queued request open and tells its visitor their place and wait within ADR 0023's one streamed answer (a waiting part before the preview), and how long a visitor may wait before the queue refuses.
  • The queue's length, given the front's memory and the 5 MB cap.
  • The estimate: crossing time from the photo's size and the measured link, plus inference time from the recent request log.
  • Whether the next photo should cross while the current one is classified.
  • Whether DINOv3, and the segmenter, can batch photos from several visitors within yappy's memory, and what that does to each visitor's wait.
  • How much traffic there is: the request log's rate of 503s and of overlapping requests.
