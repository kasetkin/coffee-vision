1. Summary

Today the page sends the original file three times, to /preview, /crop and, on a click, /classify. The backend decodes it three times and runs the segmenter twice. After this ticket the page sends the file once to one endpoint. The backend decodes it once, runs the segmenter once and uses that one result twice. It goes into the first part of the answer (the thumbnail, the mask, the box, seg_fallback) and into the classification, which follows in the second part of the same answer. The work splits into five parts:

  • classify_one split into a segment step and a classify step, which the CLI calls too.
  • The new endpoint and its streamed two-part answer (D2).
  • The page: one button, a progress circle while waiting (D3), Choose Photo disabled while a photo is in flight (D4), and D1's button.
  • The deploy's probe, warm-up and tests moved to the new endpoint, with the page and server kept compatible across a cached page and a rollback (D5).
  • The deploy (D6).

2. Background

Read at 29f3006.

  • Three uploads, three decodes, two segmenter passes. On a pick, the page posts the file to /preview (a JPEG thumbnail, long side ≤ 1024 px), then to /crop (the box, the mask, seg_fallback). Classify posts it a third time to /classify. Each decodes it with load_rgb_image; /crop (bean_region_preview) and /classify (classify_one → patches_for_photo → segment_bean_region) both segment the photo, through the same _segment helper, so the two give the same mask (webapp/app.py, coffeecv/infer.py, webapp/static/index.html).
  • Live times on yappy's GPU (ML-7 P10, 13 fixtures, second pass): /crop median 2.0 s and /classify median 9.4 s (max 11.4 s), server side. The segmenter's encoder is about 1.9 s of each (ML-7 P0's stage split, on the devcontainer; plan). One upload of the three to /classify, from the devcontainer, adds about 7 s (deploy.md). Phone upload times are not measured.
  • skip_crop is the whole photo. With skip_crop=1 the segmenter does not run and the whole original photo is classified, which is exactly ML-2 D18's fallback chosen by hand (segment_bean_region's docstring). On a fallback (seg_fallback true) the whole photo is already what was classified.
  • OPS-6's page. The overlay (tint, box, magenta outline, "Show outline") is drawn from /crop's mask by compositing (OPS-6 D3, D4, D9, D14). "Use full photo instead" appears with the overlay, before Classify, hides it, and makes Classify send skip_crop=1 (D6). A token per upload drops a stale answer (D13).
  • One request at a time. One sync gunicorn worker (--timeout 30); nginx caps the three endpoints together at one request in flight server-wide and answers a second with an immediate 503, an HTML page; proxy_read_timeout 30s. nginx buffers proxied answers by default; the template does not turn it off.
  • The request log. One JSON line per request, written by _log_request in Flask's after_request, from fields the route set on flask.g; never image bytes (docs/logging_plan.html). For a streamed answer, after_request runs before the body is sent.
  • HTTP status carries the verdict's kind. An unreadable photo is a 400; a scale or OOD refusal is a 200 with a verdict (_entry_to_response). A decode failure is found before any answer is sent.
  • The deploy and the boot name the endpoints. release_probe.py's smoke and compare, fixtures.txt, scripts/deploy_webapp.sh and the tests post to /crop and /classify; the smoke compares the mask by IoU ≥ 0.999 (OPS-6 D10). The worker's warm-up runs /crop's and /classify's whole path (ADR 0021).
  • The page is cacheable. nginx sets no Cache-Control on index.html, so a browser can hold one release's page while the server runs another (OPS-6 R6). A release ships its page and backend together (ADR 0008).
  • The orientation. load_rgb_image ignores EXIF orientation, so the thumbnail and the mask share the stored pixels; a browser's <img> applies it by default (OPS-6 R3).

3. Before and after

Now

  • Two buttons, Choose Photo and Classify.
  • Three uploads and three decodes per photo, two segmenter passes.
  • The thumbnail shows before the segmenter runs; the overlay a moment later.
  • "Use full photo instead" appears with the overlay, before classifying.
  • Endpoints /preview, /crop, /classify.

After

  • One button, Choose Photo.
  • One upload, one decode, one segmenter pass per photo.
  • A rotating progress circle until the thumbnail and the overlay come together in the answer's first part (D3); the classification follows in the second part, with no click.
  • Choose Photo is disabled while a photo is in flight (D4).
  • "Use full photo instead" appears after the classification, unless the whole photo was already used; it uploads once more with skip_crop=1 (D1).
  • One endpoint, with a streamed two-part answer (D2); index.html served no-cache (D5).

4. What does not change

  • The classification. Same model, segmenter, crop rule and patches; the second part carries the same verdicts and fields /classify answers today. classify_one is bit-identical on the fixtures before and after its split (R3).
  • The overlay. The mask's form (OPS-6 D7) and how the page draws it (D3, D4, D9, D14).
  • No state between requests. The photo lives for one request; the full-photo run is a new upload (D1). Nothing is held under a token.
  • The request log's rules. No image bytes, no client IP, one line per request.
  • One worker, one request in flight. The gunicorn and nginx caps and timeouts stay.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1Both parts reach the page together, at the end. nginx buffers proxied answers by default, and the proxy in front of the domain may too. The proxy in front is a second nginx on another server (owner, 2026-10-17). Mitigated as D11: the app sends X-Accel-Buffering: no and our nginx passes it on (proxy_pass_header; nginx hides X-Accel-* from the client by default), so both nginx servers stream the answer. The live check reports when each part arrived; the owner judges it on a phone (D6).
R2The request log loses its classify fields and its latency. Flask's after_request runs before a streamed body is sent, so _log_request would write its line before the classification has set its fields. Mitigated by writing the line when the stream ends, with an app test that the line has the classify fields and the whole latency.
R3Splitting classify_one changes an answer, or the CLI and the service drift. coffeecv/infer.py is shared with the CLI and, through segment_bean_region, with training. Mitigated by one segment step and one classify step that classify_one itself calls, so the CLI and the service run the same code, and by checking classify_one bit-identical on the fixtures (as OPS-6 R5).
R4A failure after the first part has no HTTP status. The status goes out with the first part. An unreadable photo is still a 400, found before it. A later error is sent as an error part; a stream that ends with no second part (a dropped connection, the worker killed at 30 s) is shown as "could not reach the server", not left waiting.
R5The photo shows later than today. Today the thumbnail comes before the segmenter; after, the first thing shown waits for the upload, the decode and about 2 s of segmenter. The 2 s is server time; the upload on a phone is not measured. Accepted in D3: a rotating progress circle covers the wait, and the upload is not measured.
R6The worker is busy longer for each photo. Every pick now costs a classification, about 9 s on the GPU, in one request, also when the user would have stopped at the mask. A second visitor meets nginx's 503 for that long; the same visitor cannot pick again until the answer is in (D4). Accepted at today's traffic; the request log shows it (preview_ms and latency_ms, D13), and the page says the server is busy (D12). A queue, or batching, for several visitors is OPS-8.
R7A cached page meets a server with the other API. After the deploy, a cached old page posts to /preview; after a --rollback, a cached new page posts to the new endpoint. Mitigated by D5: Cache-Control: no-cache makes the browser revalidate the page on every load, so it always gets the page of the release the server runs. Not for a page cached before this deploy, which has no no-cache yet: until it is reloaded, it answers "Could not read this photo." Accepted, once (2026-10-17).
R8A rollback across this release takes the site down. Found while planning. The nginx site is not part of a release: render_production installs it from the release being deployed, and rollback_links only switches the links (scripts/deploy_webapp.sh). After this deploy the site routes /analyze only, so the previous release's page fails behind it, and --verify posts /analyze to a backend that does not have it. This covers the deploy's own rollback on a failed verify too. Accepted (D10, D16): fixed forward by a new deploy if anything goes wrong; a release that carries its own nginx site is OPS-9.
R9A slow phone holds the worker. Found in review. With X-Accel-Buffering: no, nginx reads the answer from the backend only up to proxy_buffer_size (4k or 8k by default) ahead of what the client has taken. The preview line is a few hundred KB (a 1024 px JPEG and a PNG, base64), so the sync worker blocks writing it until the phone has read most of it, and the classification starts only after that write. A client that stops reading keeps the worker until gunicorn's 30 s timeout kills it, and the next worker boots for about 20 s (ML-7). Today nginx buffers the whole answer, so neither happens. Mitigated by D18. Measured 2026-10-17 in P3, on loopback through two nginx servers: the worker was not held with D18's buffer or without it, since the kernel's socket buffers took the whole 215 KB answer; D18 stays as a margin, and the real path (a phone, the proxy in front, our nginx) is not measured.
R10A streamed answer that fails looks like a success in the logs. Found in review. The status goes out with the first part (R4), so the request log and nginx's access log both say 200 whether the answer completed, ended in an error part, or lost its client after the preview. A worker killed at 30 s writes no request-log line at all. Mitigated by D19.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • The page has one button, Choose Photo; a pick uploads the photo once, and the classification needs no click.
  • One decode and one segmenter pass per photo; classify_one bit-identical on the fixtures before and after (R3).
  • The first part (thumbnail, mask, box, seg_fallback) reaches the page before the classification ends, on the dev server and through nginx on the live domain (R1).
  • The overlay looks as after OPS-6; the second part's verdicts and fields are /classify's.
  • "Use full photo instead" as D1: after the answer, not after a skip_crop=1 answer or a fallback, and one more upload with skip_crop=1.
  • One request-log line per request, with the classify fields, the whole latency and preview_ms (R2, D13), and outcome and parts_sent; nginx's access log ends each line with $upstream_bytes_received (D19, R10).
  • nginx takes the whole answer from the backend at once (proxy_buffer_size 1m): through two local nginx servers, a throttled client gets the preview first and does not lengthen the classification (D18, R9).
  • An error after the first part, and a stream cut before the second, each show a message (R4).
  • A rotating progress circle shows from the pick until the first part, then under the preview until the second part (D3).
  • Choose Photo, and D1's button, are disabled while a photo is in flight (D4).
  • A 503 shows the busy message (D12).
  • index.html is served with Cache-Control: no-cache, and /preview, /crop and /classify are gone (D5); the one endpoint is /analyze (D7).
  • The deploy's smoke and compare, the fixtures, the warm-up (ADR 0021) and the tests use the new endpoint; the smoke still compares the mask by IoU ≥ 0.999; --verify reports when each part arrived through the domain (D11).
  • webapp/README.md and deploy.md name the new endpoint.
  • The owner has seen the page on the local dev server and accepted it.
  • Deployed and checked on the live site (D6).

Out of scope

  • Holding the photo on the server between requests (D2 (b)).
  • Measuring phone upload times (D3).
  • Shrinking or re-encoding the photo on the phone before upload: the classifier gets the original pixels (the parity rule in CODING_STANDARDS.md).
  • Showing portrait photos upright (EXIF orientation), an idea from the stub.
  • Editing the mask or the crop box; the TODO(crop editing) in index.html stays, though it assumed a box edited before classifying.
  • Going back from the whole photo's answer to the masked one without a new pick (D1).
  • More workers, or a different nginx cap; a queue or batching for several visitors (OPS-8).
  • A rollback across this release, and a release that carries its nginx site (OPS-9, D10); an undo procedure (D16); a local rehearsal (D17).
