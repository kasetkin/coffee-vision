Ticket ML-7 · coffee-vision · inference runtime

Serve the segmenter and the classifier from ONNX Runtime or LiteRT; training stays on PyTorch

Both models run in PyTorch eager mode on the CPU today. The live site takes 20–27 s per photo on a 4-core host behind a 30 s timeout. This ticket converts the shipped XL0 segmenter and the shipped DINOv3 ViT-B/16 classifier to a dedicated inference runtime, ONNX Runtime or LiteRT (the renamed TensorFlow Lite), and serves the converted copies. Training stays on PyTorch: the crops, the embeddings, the head, the OOD reference and the probe (D3). A model is trained, then converted, checked against its PyTorch self, and deployed. The live host, yappy, has an Intel iGPU with OpenCL, and the owner wants LiteRT tested on it (D8). The research is done and summarised below. D2 waits on the comparison, and D4's int8 question on its numbers; every other decision is made. Planned, not implemented.

1. Summary

Today both models run as PyTorch modules everywhere: the vendored EfficientViT-SAM-XL0 with the fine-tuned xl0_v3 decoder, and the timm DINOv3 ViT-B/16 with its folded logistic-regression head. After this ticket, training is unchanged, and the release route gains a conversion step. The shipped models are exported to one runtime, chosen by the owner on measurements from the live host. Each export is checked against the PyTorch original (D7), and the webapp serves it. The parity that CODING_STANDARDS.md asks for (the same crop, mask and normalisation in training and serving) then rests on that check rather than on shared code.

The work splits into five parts:

  1. Measure. Time each stage on the live host.
  2. Spike. Export both models and test them for parity and speed.
  3. Choose. The owner picks the runtime on that evidence.
  4. Convert in the release route. A DVC stage exports a shipped model and checks it, and the serving path in coffeecv/infer.py runs the export.
  5. Deploy. Ship the current model's converted copy and report the latency.

2. Background

2.1 What runs today

Read at 69d9870.

  • Segmenter. segment_and_crop (coffeecv/segment_beans.py) runs three steps:
    1. Resize, normalise and pad the photo to [1,3,1024,1024].
    2. Encode it to a [1,256,64,64] embedding.
    3. Decode a whole-image box prompt to three low-resolution masks of 256×256 and keep multi3 (index 2).
    Then it upsamples bilinearly to the photo's size and thresholds at 0. The encoder is the frozen XL0 base (468 MB, 117 M parameters). Only the mask decoder is fine-tuned: models/seg/xl0_v3.pt, 16 MB, about 4 M parameters.
  • Classifier. Each photo gives 40 bean-unit patches, resized to 224 px and ImageNet-normalised. They run in batches of 32 and 8 through vit_base_patch16_dinov3 (timm 1.0.30, RoPE, 4 register tokens). The model reads out cls_mean, a 1536-d vector, and applies a Linear(1536, 10) with the scaler folded in (coffeecv/backbones.py, coffeecv/linear_head.py). The same 1536-d vectors feed the OOD guard's centroid score and its probe (coffeecv/infer.py).
  • What stays on PyTorch (D3). These paths fit or produce what serving is measured against, and none of them moves:
    • the segcrop stage (coffeecv/segcrop_session.py), which cuts the training pools;
    • fit_frozen_head.py, which embeds them and fits the head;
    • build_ood_reference.py, ood_eval.py and fit_ood_probe.py, which embed through forward_with_embeddings;
    • the decoder fine-tune (seg_finetune.py).
    tests/test_seg_parity.py pins segcrop's arrays to serving's, array for array. Under this ticket it must compare the PyTorch path with the runtime path at D7's tolerance instead.
  • The earlier decision. ML-2 kept the PyTorch runtime "for now, at both ends". It said a later move "has to switch at both ends together (training pools rebuilt too), because two runtimes would break train/inference parity" (ML-2 plan Q4; ML-2 D23 and R5). D3 replaces that rule. Parity is kept by checking every export against its PyTorch original, not by running one runtime at both ends. The onnx import is patched out of the vendored copy (third_party/efficientvit/PATCHES.md, row 3). tests/test_sam_loader.py checks that the segmenter builds with onnx blocked, and neither lock installs it.

2.2 What it costs

  • Live site. 20.1–26.7 s per photo on webappremote (4 cores, 7.5 GB), against gunicorn's and nginx's 30 s timeouts. At 2 cores the smoke test went over 30 s (c30688f; ML-6 activity). Each upload runs the segmenter twice, once for /crop and once for /classify (OPS-7).
  • What is already timed. The app logs decode_ms, crop_detect_ms and inference_ms with each request's latency_ms (webapp/app.py), so a coarse split of the live time exists but has never been summarised. The segmenter also times its resize, encoder, decoder and upsample (timing_ms in coffeecv/segment_beans.py); that split is not logged.
  • The compute ceiling. Estimates from public specifications, not measurements (D14). The N100's CPU peaks at about 190 GFLOPS fp32 (4 cores, 16 FLOP per cycle, about 2.9 GHz on all cores), its iGPU at about 290 (24 EUs, 16 FLOP per cycle, 750 MHz), or about 580 in fp16. They share a 6 W package budget and one memory bus. DINOv3 B/16 is about 17.6 GMAC per patch, so 40 patches are about 1.4 TFLOP: at least 7.5 s on this CPU at full efficiency. The 5.8 s below, from another machine, likely understates yappy. In fp32 the iGPU has at most about 1.5× the CPU's peak.
  • Stage times measured elsewhere. None of these was taken on webappremote, an Intel N100 with AVX2 and AVX-VNNI but no AVX-512 or AMX (2.4).
    • XL0 encoder: 2.3 s at 4 threads on the devcontainer (Ryzen 5 5600U, AVX2); decoder 0.13–0.16 s (analysis/ml5_xl0_timing/).
    • DINOv3 B/16: 145 ms per patch at 4 threads on the retired VM, so about 5.8 s for 40 patches (docs/dinov3_integration_plan.md §6.2).
  • Footprint. The torch 2.14.0 CPU wheel is 196 MB. The deploy preflight wants 5 GB of available memory, and the ML-6 deploy fell short and was rerun at 4 GB (6842b08). The webapp never sets a thread count, and gunicorn runs one sync worker.
  • Deploy gates that pin numbers.
    • deploy_webapp.sh --compare wants bit-identical answers from the training and release environments. Once the release runs the runtime, that cannot hold (R4).
    • release_probe.py wants scores within 1e-4 and mask IoU of at least 0.999.
    • tests/test_dino_backbone.py pins ViT-S/16 readouts against Meta's code at atol 1e-4.

2.3 The two runtimes

Research, 2026-10-13, from primary sources. The facts that decide the choice were checked twice: the PyPI metadata and torch 2.14's opset constants.

ONNX RuntimeLiteRT
Runtime onnxruntime 1.31.0, MIT, cp312 x86_64 wheel about 24 MB. Built for server CPUs as well as edge devices. ai-edge-litert 2.3.0, Apache-2.0, cp312 x86_64 wheel about 22 MB, no TensorFlow. PyPI describes it as "for mobile and embedded devices"; its CPU backend is XNNPACK.
Converter torch.onnx.export(dynamo=True), part of torch 2.14 (needs onnx and onnxscript). Default opset 20, dynamic batch through dynamic_shapes, torch.onnx.verification for parity checks. litert-torch 0.9.4, formerly ai-edge-torch. It requires torch<2.14, so it needs its own conversion environment. It converts static shapes only: dynamic batch is an open bug (litert-torch #870).
DINOv3 timm's DINOv3 RoPE is real sin/cos with no complex numbers, and no ONNX bug was found against it. Whether torch.export traces it is untested. Kornia's DINOv3 ViT-S export matched PyTorch at cosine 0.999998 in fp32. An onnx-community ViT-B/16 export exists, but it is the Hugging Face port, not timm's. Community conversions of timm DINOv3 ViT-S and DINOv2 needed graph rewrites: 4-D attention and constant RoPE tables, for mobile GPUs. The Hugging Face DINOv3 failed on an attention op (#827). No CPU parity report exists for B/16.
XL0 segmenter Upstream ships ONNX export scripts for the encoder and decoder, already in third_party/efficientvit/applications/efficientvit_sam/deployment/onnx/. They use the legacy exporter at opset 17. The decoder takes the box as two points labelled 2 and 3. Its --return-single-mask picks by argmax IoU, which is not multi3. No EfficientViT-SAM port exists. The neck's UpSampleLayer is bicubic, which litert-torch cannot lower. Upstream's TFLite script swaps it for bilinear, which would change the model.
Speed on x86 No careful ViT-B comparison against torch 2.x exists. The evidence points to about 1.0–1.5× in fp32. Dynamic int8 gave 2.6× on a 4-vCPU Ice Lake in 2022, an older stack. No native x86 benchmark against ORT or PyTorch for ViT-class models. A 2023 study found TFLite slowest on Intel CPUs, but it predates today's XNNPACK.
In the browser onnxruntime-web (WASM, WebGPU); transformers.js already runs DINOv3. LiteRT.js (WebGPU, WASM, 2 GB memory cap); a SAM 2.1-Tiny demo runs.

Facts that hold for both runtimes:

  • fp16 is no option on this CPU. ORT's CPU build has no fp16 kernels, and DINOv3's attention logits reach about 2.1e6, past fp16's 65,504, which gives NaN.
  • int8 moves the embeddings. A DINOv2-based retrieval model fell from R@1 89.9 to 62.6 under int8. With training on PyTorch (D3), the head and the probe were fitted on fp32 embeddings, so int8 serving would feed them inputs they were not fitted on (D4).
  • An exported DINOv3 file is a derivative work under the DINOv3 License, like the .pt. It may live in DVC; published weights would need the licence shipped with them.
  • OpenVINO reads ONNX and may be fastest on Intel CPUs. It is measured as ORT's execution provider, on the GPU (D9) and on the CPU (D14), not as a runtime of its own.

2.4 The live host's iGPU, and LiteRT's GPU paths

webappremote is the host yappy (the SSH config). The owner's clinfo, 2026-10-13, shows:

  • Device. Intel Graphics [0x46d1]: Alder Lake-N, as in the N100 class. 24 compute units at 750 MHz, with fp16 and DP4A.
  • Driver. OpenCL 3.0 on Intel's NEO driver 22.43.24595, from late 2022. It lists 0x46d1.
  • Memory. 6 GiB global memory, at most 3 GiB per allocation. The memory is shared with the host's 7.5 GB of RAM.

Read on the host by Claude over SSH, 2026-10-13 (lscpu, vulkaninfo --summary):

  • CPU. Intel N100: 4 cores, 1 thread each, up to 3.4 GHz, 6 MiB L3. It has AVX2, FMA, F16C, AVX-VNNI (the 256-bit form) and VAES. It has no AVX-512 and no AMX, so ORT's AVX-512 VNNI and AMX int8 paths do not apply. AVX-VNNI does: ORT's MLAS swaps in AVX-VNNI int8 GEMM and convolution kernels when the CPU reports it (platform.cpp, read 2026-10-13), so int8 has a fast path here (D4, D14).
  • System. Debian 12, kernel 6.1, 7,681 MB of RAM, about 4.9 GB available at the time.
  • Vulkan. Mesa 22.3.6's Intel driver (ANV) shows the iGPU as "Intel(R) Graphics (ADL-N)", Vulkan 1.3.230. Only sudo sees it. The deploy user is not in the render group, /dev/dri/renderD128 is root:render mode 660, and without access Vulkan offers only llvmpipe, Mesa's software renderer on the CPU.

Research, 2026-10-13, on what LiteRT can do with that GPU:

  • The pip wheel has no OpenCL path. ai-edge-litert 2.3.0 for Linux x86_64 ships libLiteRtWebGpuAccelerator.so and no OpenCL accelerator (wheel listing, checked here). The WebGPU accelerator runs through Dawn on Vulkan, so on yappy it would use Mesa's Vulkan driver, not the OpenCL driver clinfo shows. The release notes of 2.1.2 say "Desktop GPU support in the ai-edge-litert Python package for Linux (through WebGPU)".
  • OpenCL means a source build. LiteRT builds libLiteRtGpuAccelerator.so (OpenCL and WebGPU, OpenCL preferred) or libLiteRtOpenClAccelerator.so from source with Bazel, and its loader looks for both before the WebGPU library (gpu_registry.cc). Nobody has reported that a self-built accelerator works with the 2.3.0 wheel. It needs the unversioned libOpenCL.so.
  • The OpenCL path and Intel GPUs. LiteRT's GPU engine (ML Drift) has Intel-specific code, and its paper benchmarks Meteor Lake and Lunar Lake over OpenCL, about 2× faster than WebGPU on one model. No report covers Linux, the NEO driver or Alder Lake-N.
  • The GPU defaults to fp16. GpuOptions(enforce_f32=True) forces fp32. Without it, DINOv3's attention logits overflow, and LiteRT returns NaN with no error, still reporting the model "fully accelerated" (LiteRT #9249).
  • Op coverage. The GPU handles tensors of up to 4 dimensions. A 5-D reshape, like the qkv split in timm's attention, falls back to the CPU, and split execution is usually slower; is_fully_accelerated() tells. The ops needed here are supported: batch matmul, softmax, GELU, sin, cos, slicing, mean and bilinear resize. Bicubic is not a TFLite op.
  • Start-up. Python cannot set the GPU's program-cache options, so every process start compiles all kernels again.
  • The sandbox. The service runs as its own user under a systemd sandbox (OPS-1). The GPU needs:
    • /dev/dri/renderD128 and the render group;
    • its kernel caches (NEO's, or Mesa's for Vulkan) moved off the home directory, which ProtectHome=true hides, or turned off.
    ioctl is in @system-service, so the syscall filter allows it.
  • The same iGPU through Intel's stack. OpenVINO's GPU plugin uses the NEO OpenCL driver directly, with an fp32 precision hint and a kernel cache directory. It can be reached as openvino 2026.4.1, as ONNX Runtime's OpenVINO execution provider (onnxruntime-openvino 1.24.1), or through LiteRT's own OpenVINO dispatch library, which the wheel ships. Whether OpenVINO 2026 still supports driver 22.43 is unverified.

3. Before and after

Now

  • Both models run as PyTorch modules, in training and serving.
  • A shipped model goes straight from fit_frozen_head --ship to the deploy.
  • No onnx or runtime package in either environment; its import is patched out.
  • The release environment carries torch, torchvision, timm and segment-anything.
  • About 20–27 s per photo on the live host. The log splits it into decode, crop detection and inference, but nobody has summarised that split, and the model stages are not timed on the host.

After

  • Training is unchanged and stays on PyTorch.
  • The release route gains a step: a DVC stage exports the shipped model and checks the export against PyTorch (D6, D7). A model whose export fails the check does not deploy.
  • The webapp runs the XL0 encoder, the XL0 decoder and the DINOv3 backbone with its readout as exported graphs on one runtime. The cards record each file's sha256 and the runtime version.
  • The training environment gains the exporter; the release environment gains the runtime, and loses torch only if D5 says so.
  • Per-stage times on the live host, measured before and after.

4. What does not change

  • Training. The segcrop stage, the decoder fine-tune, the embeddings for the head, the head fit, the OOD reference and the probe stay on PyTorch and scikit-learn (D3). No re-crop and no retrain come from this ticket.
  • The models. They stay DINOv3 ViT-B/16 with cls_mean and a C=0.1 linear head (ADR 0006), and the XL0 segmenter at 1024 px with a box prompt and multi3 (ADR 0015). The export must reproduce them, bicubic neck included, not approximate them.
  • Pre- and post-processing. The resize, normalisation, patch sampling, mask upsampling and D18 fallback stay as they are, unless D5 moves them out of torch. In that case they must match the torch versions to D7's tolerance.
  • Releases and environments. Releases stay immutable, one per directory (ADR 0008). New packages come through uv lock (ADR 0007).
  • The OOD guard. Its logic and fixed 0.5 probe threshold stay (ADR 0016).

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1No speedup in fp32. The sources suggest 1.0–1.5× for ORT and give no x86 figure for LiteRT. The live host is a 4-core N100 with AVX2 and AVX-VNNI and no AVX-512 (2.4), and its iGPU's fp32 peak is about 1.5× the CPU's (2.2). Mitigation: plan P0 measures each stage on webappremote, at the release's thread count, before any conversion, and stops for the owner if the models are under half of each photo's time. ORT's OpenVINO provider on the CPU is measured beside the GPU cells (D14). The owner sets the gain worth the switch (D2, D15).
R2Serving drifts from what training fitted. The pools were cut and the head and probe fitted on PyTorch outputs (D3), and serving would now compute them on another runtime. In fp32 the gap is rounding: thresholded masks flip edge pixels, and probabilities and guard scores shift by small amounts. A photo near the guard's 0.5 threshold or near a top-1 tie can change answer. Mitigation: D7's gate on every export, on fixtures and validation photos, with the photos that change answer listed for the owner. fp32 only unless D4 says otherwise.
R3The export fails or is silently wrong. Known traps:
  • DINOv3 RoPE through torch.export is untested.
  • Upstream's decoder export selects masks differently from multi3.
  • The bicubic neck under LiteRT.
  • LiteRT's static batch: 40 patches would need fixed batch signatures.
  • A SAM 2.1 LiteRT decoder once converted "fine" and gave masks at correlation 0.265.
Mitigation: export with the dynamo exporter first. The bicubic neck and the 5-D qkv split are rewritten exactly in the export wrappers, the neck as two matmuls with torch's own coefficients, and checked against torch before export (D14). Gate on the readout's cosine and max-abs difference, mask IoU, top-1 and guard verdicts on real photos, not on "it converts".
R4The deploy gates assume one runtime. --compare demands bit-identical answers between the training environment (PyTorch) and the release (the runtime). That can no longer hold. release_probe.py compares the smoke test's answers with locally computed ones. ORT's saved ORT_ENABLE_ALL graphs are tied to one ISA, and the devcontainer (AVX2) and the host may differ. Mitigation: --compare checks the release against the local runtime path bit for bit, and against PyTorch at D7's tolerance. Graphs are optimised at session load, not in the stored file.
R5Memory on a 7.5 GB host. The ML-6 deploy already fell short of the preflight's 5 GB. ORT's arena, or torch and a runtime loaded side by side under D5 (a), could make it worse. Mitigation: peak RSS measured in plan P0, in plan P4's photo replay with both models loaded, and at the smoke test.
R6The iGPU path is unproven and costly to keep. OpenCL needs a LiteRT accelerator built from source. It has to match the wheel it plugs into, and is rebuilt on every LiteRT upgrade. The 2022 driver, Alder Lake-N and Linux are all unreported. Silent wrong answers come from fp16 by default and from partial CPU fallback. Kernels compile at every start. GPU memory is the host's RAM (R5). Mitigation: P1 tests it before any integration, with enforce_f32, is_fully_accelerated() and D7's gate. The time and peak RSS count from a cold start. The sandbox changes are tried in the smoke-test unit first. A WebGPU run that cannot open the GPU may land on llvmpipe, Mesa's CPU renderer, without failing (2.4). Each GPU run logs the adapter it got and refuses llvmpipe, and proves the GPU computed with the i915 counters in sysfs (frequency and idle time), not only that the device was opened (D14).

  • The owner chooses the runtime (D2). One ADR records D2 and D3, since they bind later tickets: models train on PyTorch and are served as checked exports on that runtime. It names what it changes in ML-2 Q4 and in ADR 0015's "same code in training and serving", and CODING_STANDARDS.md's parity rule is updated to match.
  • The export and its check run as a DVC stage from the shipped checkpoints (D6). The serving path in coffeecv/infer.py runs the exports, and the PyTorch path stays for training. tests/test_seg_parity.py compares the two at D7's tolerance, and the onnx import test and PATCHES.md row 3 are updated.
  • experiments.md and deploy.md name the conversion step between shipping a candidate and deploying it. The deploy gates (--compare, release_probe.py) check the release against the runtime path and against PyTorch (R4).
  • If the iGPU is chosen, the service unit gets the GPU device and the render group, and its kernel caches go to a writable directory or are turned off. Every other sandbox line stays. webapp/README.md names the host's GPU driver as a deploy dependency.
  • The serving path loads and warms both models at worker start. Both load there today (webapp/app.py), but neither is warmed, so the first request pays the first inference's set-up. The worker's boot time, kernel compilation included, is reported against gunicorn's timeout.
  • The current model's converted copy is deployed and the gates pass. The latency_ms from the app's log is reported before and after.

Out of scope

  • Moving any training path to the runtime: segcrop, embeddings, head, reference, probe, decoder fine-tune (D3).
  • Inference in the visitor's browser. It would be its own ticket if D1 (c) is wanted.
  • GPU serving and TensorRT (ML-2, out of scope).
  • OpenVINO as a runtime of its own, torch.compile and AOTInductor. They were not requested; a ticket of their own if the comparison disappoints. OpenVINO is measured as ORT's execution provider, on the GPU and the CPU (D9, D14), and serving through it would be ORT under D2.
  • Upgrading yappy's GPU driver. If the 2022 driver blocks the test, that goes to the owner first.
  • Running the segmenter once per upload instead of twice (OPS-7). The plan's report shows what it would save on each cell, beside the runtime's own gain.
  • Changing either model, its input size or its patch count.
