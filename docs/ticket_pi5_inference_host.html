Ticket OPS-23 · coffee-vision · inference host

Measure the Raspberry Pi 5 as a replacement inference host, on its CPU and its GPU

The owner asked whether the Home Assistant Raspberry Pi 5 could take over model inference from yappy, running the models in Docker. Both paths were measured on 2026-11-10 with ML-7's photo replay and its D7 check against PyTorch, on LiteRT, PyTorch and ONNX Runtime. Every fp32 path passes D7, but the fastest, ONNX Runtime on the CPU, needs about 24 s of model time for a photo, and the fastest GPU path, LiteRT, about 104 s, where yappy's GPU needs about 8 s. With DINOv3 rewritten so that XNNPACK really runs it in fp16, the Pi's CPU needs about 14 s per photo and stays within ADR 0020's fp16 bar on 288 photos. Measured; nothing is planned or deployed.

Owner request, 2026-11-10. Connect to the Home Assistant Raspberry Pi 5 (HA OS, ssh alias webapphaos) and see whether the project's models can run there on the CPU inside a Docker image, to replace yappy for inference. Then: compare the Pi with yappy's Intel N100; then try the Pi's GPU through Mesa, Vulkan and LiteRT in fp32, with the latest Mesa, building it overnight if needed. Then: record the CPU and GPU results as a ticket. Then: check the ONNX path on the Pi, CPU and GPU, against LiteRT and PyTorch. Then (2026-11-10): continue the Pi experiments and report how inference on the Pi can be sped up with fp16 arithmetic; do not report the XNNPACK bug found on the way.

1. Summary

The Pi 5 cannot replace yappy without making answers several times slower. Its CPU, on LiteRT with XNNPACK in fp32, needs about 29 s of model time per photo as served today, and passes D7. fp16 on its CPU, with today's graphs, either returns NaN or quietly runs at fp32 speed. With DINOv3's means rewritten (below) it runs at 2.5× DINOv3's fp32 speed, and with the segmenter left in fp32 a served photo takes about 14 s, within ADR 0020's fp16 bar on 288 photos. Its GPU (V3D 7.1) runs the converted models through LiteRT's WebGPU accelerator only with a Vulkan layer that hides two of V3DV's gaps from Dawn, and only with Mesa 26.2. It then passes D7 but needs about 104 s per photo, 3.6× slower than the Pi's own CPU. ONNX Runtime is the fastest runtime on the Pi's CPU, at about 24 s per photo and passing D7 (16% below LiteRT, 10% below PyTorch); its WebGPU plugin on the Pi's GPU needs about 10 minutes per photo. yappy's GPU needs about 8–9 s (ADR 0019).

2. Background

  • The host. Raspberry Pi 5, 8 GB, 4 Cortex-A76 cores with hardware fp16 (fphp, asimdhp), HA OS 18.5, Docker 29.8.2 reached through the Advanced SSH & Web Terminal app (protection mode off, user hassio with sudo). About 6.3 GB of memory was available beside Home Assistant's containers, which kept running throughout. No fan; the CPU reached 84 °C under sustained load, with the reported clock at 2.4 GHz throughout (firmware throttle flags not read: no vcgencmd in the app).
  • The instrument. ML-7's photo replay (analysis/ml7_gpu/p4_replay.py: 13 photos, the segmenter's encoder and decoder twice per photo, then DINOv3 on the photo's 40 patches; the first photo, cold, left out), its per-graph timing (p4_graph.py) and its D7 check (parity.py, through analysis/pi5/parity_now.py, which fits it to today's BeanSegmenter), on ML-7's inputs and PyTorch references (analysis/ml7_gpu/artifacts/, checkpoint allrigs_dino3b16_segxl0v3_country_s42). The CPU runs use ML-7 P2's .tflite files, the ones behind ADR 0019's yappy numbers; the fp16 DINOv3 and the GPU runs use the release's files (models/allrigs_dino3b16_segxl0v3_country_s42.litert/), which carry ADR 0022's forms.
  • The containers. python:3.12-slim on arm64: ai-edge-litert==2.3.0 (the aarch64 wheel ships libLiteRt.so and libLiteRtWebGpuAccelerator.so); torch==2.14.0+cpu from PyTorch's CPU index (PyPI's aarch64 wheel pulls in the CUDA stack); onnxruntime==1.31.0 and onnxruntime-ep-webgpu==0.4.0, ML-7's versions, both with aarch64 wheels, on ML-7 P2's ONNX files (analysis/ml7_gpu/artifacts/onnx/); for the GPU, trixie with Mesa 25.0.7, 26.1.6 from trixie-backports, and 26.2.6 from sid.
  • A served photo runs the encoder and decoder once (one streamed request, ADR 0023) and DINOv3 on 40 patches at batch 8. The replay runs the segmenter twice, as serving did at ML-7, so the per-photo serving estimates below are summed from the per-graph times.

The GPU

  • The device. /dev/dri/renderD128 is v3d (V3D 7.1). Mesa's V3DV reports Vulkan 1.3. Debian bookworm's Mesa 22.3 predates the Pi 5's GPU (Mesa 23.3), so the image is trixie. The container also sees llvmpipe; VK_ICD_FILENAMES pins Broadcom's driver. Mesa 26 opens the display node as well, so the container needs all of /dev/dri.
  • Dawn refuses V3DV. LiteRT's WebGPU accelerator (Dawn) rejects the adapter with Vulkan shaderUniform*ArrayDynamicIndexing required: V3DV reports all four shader*ArrayDynamicIndexing features false. With those reported, Dawn then rejects WebGPU core's limits: 4096 against 8192 for 1D, 2D and cube textures, viewports and framebuffers, 4 fragment outputs against 8, and the inter-stage variables. All of these concern rendering, not compute. No environment variable changes this; the V3D_WEBGPU_OVERRIDE found online appears in neither Mesa's driver nor LiteRT's library. The same rejection is reported for another Dawn application on the Pi 5, unresolved (dusklight #2592).
  • The workaround. analysis/pi5/pishim.c, a Vulkan layer enabled only inside the test container (VK_INSTANCE_LAYERS=VK_LAYER_PI_webgpu_shim), reports the four features (WGSL has no descriptor arrays, so compute shaders cannot use them) and strips them from vkCreateDevice; with PISHIM_LIMITS=1 it reports the render limits at core's values. Dawn then selects V3D 7.1 … backend=Vulkan and LiteRT places every operation of the release's graphs on the GPU. With PISHIM_DUMP it saves each SPIR-V module Dawn creates.
  • Mesa 25.0.7 (trixie) never compiles the decoder. Its 42nd module (36 KB of SPIR-V; a softmax with a 32 × 4 local array of vec2 and many exp calls) stayed in Mesa's generic NIR optimisation loop (nir_opt_algebraic, nir_opt_cse under v3d_optimize_nir, read with Debian's debug symbols) for more than 20 minutes. V3D_DEBUG=opt_compile_time,noloopunroll did not help.
  • Mesa 26.1.6 (trixie-backports) compiles every graph in about 4 minutes, but the encoder returns NaN or Inf on all 5 calls.
  • Mesa 26.2.6 (sid) is correct.
  • ONNX Runtime's WebGPU plugin (also Dawn) needs the same layer and Mesa, and one more step: ORT 1.31's own device discovery reads /sys/class/drm/card*/device/vendor, a PCI attribute, and skips non-PCI cards other than NVIDIA's Jetson (Skipping non-PCI DRM card), so it offers the plugin no GPU. The test container mounts a fake /sys/class/drm (card0/device/vendor 0x14e4, device 0x2712, the V3D's uevent); Dawn and Mesa still open the real device through /dev/dri. ORT then lists WebGpuExecutionProvider on a GPU.

fp16 on the CPU

The A76 has fp16 arithmetic, and XNNPACK uses it when LiteRT passes flag 4 (FORCE_FP16). DINOv3 is about 80% of a served photo's time on the Pi's CPU, so the work went into it. Scripts and records: analysis/pi5/fp16/.

  • XNNPACK converts a graph to fp16 entirely or not at all, and says nothing when it does not. Graphs with ADR 0022's forms ran at fp32 speed under flag 4. The cause is the scaled LayerNorm's two .mean() calls, which share one constant 1/768. While widening fp16 sums' accumulators to fp32 (widen_fp16_accumulators), XNNPACK reads that constant after replacing its buffer, so the rewrite fails and the graph runs in fp32. On one block, two means kept fp16 to 1.10× fp32's speed; the same block with its means as sums ran at 2.30×. ADR 0022's x86 fp16 checks may also have run partly in fp32. The owner decided not to report the bug upstream.
  • On the A76, XNNPACK's fp16 sums add up in fp16. With every mean written as sum(x × 1/N) (allpre), the rewrite succeeds and runs at 1.88 s per call, but gives NaN: block 0's output already has a cosine of 0.78. On x86 the same kernels add up in fp32.
  • The form that works (lnmean). Every mean goes through TFLite's MEAN operation, which XNNPACK widens correctly. The LayerNorm becomes F.layer_norm(x / max|x|), and the patch mean of the readout becomes an AVERAGE_POOL_2D over the 14 × 14 grid. The form is exact except for eps. F.layer_norm takes a constant, so eps is 1e-12 instead of eps × r², a departure from ADR 0022. eps is far below every token's variance, and in fp32 the graph passes D7: least cosine 0.9999992.
  • Splitting fc2's sum in two (lnmean_split768) brings the answers within the fp16 bar at no measurable cost. fc2 has 3072 inputs; the split adds four 768-wide sums in a tree, as ADR 0022 does for the encoder's convolutions. Splitting every Linear at 128 is closer to fp32 but slower.
  • The segmenter stays in fp32. P2's encoder returns NaN in fp16. The encoder with ADR 0022's fp16 forms runs at 2.35 s in fp16, against 4.17 s for P2's encoder in fp32. On the replay, its masks fall to an IoU of 0.99675 against the 0.999 bar. The x86-only fault in conv → GELU → conv that ML-7 found does not occur on the A76.
  • ONNX Runtime with ML-7's mixed-fp16 DINOv3 takes 9.2 s per call on the Pi's CPU, slower than its fp32 at 3.69 s, with least cosine 0.99907.

A served photo with the segmenter in fp32 (P2's encoder 4.17 s, decoder 0.12 s) and lnmean_split768 in fp16 takes about 14 s. 3. Before and after

Now

  • yappy serves both models on its GPU, about 8–9 s per photo.
  • Whether the Pi 5 could serve was unknown.

After

  • The Pi 5 is measured: on its CPU about 24 s per photo with ONNX Runtime, 27 s with PyTorch and 29 s with LiteRT, all fp32 and passing D7; on its GPU about 104 s with LiteRT, passing D7, and about 10 minutes with ONNX Runtime's WebGPU plugin; with DINOv3 in fp16 in the lnmean_split768 form, about 14 s on its CPU, within ADR 0020's fp16 bar on 288 photos.
  • The scripts, the Vulkan layer and the result records are in analysis/pi5/, the fp16 work in analysis/pi5/fp16/.

4. What does not change

  • Serving. yappy keeps serving; no release, deploy or code path changes.
  • Containerised serving stays closed (.out-of-scope/containerised-serving.md). The Pi could only serve from a container on HA OS, so serving there would reopen that decision; nothing here proposes it.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The Pi's numbers are measured beside Home Assistant, without a fan. Other containers kept running and the CPU reached 84 °C. The fp32 replay's photos varied by about 1 s once the copy stopped; the conclusion holds with a large margin either way.
R2The GPU result depends on a layer that misreports the device. It reports features and limits V3DV lacks. D7 passed on all 13 photos, but a kernel that used a reported texture size beyond 4096 would fail on the device. ONNX Runtime's GPU run adds a fake /sys/class/drm. Both are kept to benchmark containers; neither is a serving path.
R3The fp16 result rests on 288 photos and on XNNPACK's behaviour. ADR 0022 sizes fp16 forms over all 2,027 photos in dataset/. Here 288 were checked, 107 of them served, and the least served cosine (0.99969) is close to the bar. XNNPACK falls back to fp32 without saying so; a LiteRT upgrade that fixes or moves the fault changes the speed, and possibly the arithmetic. Before serving it, the form would be held to the full range scan and the bar on every photo, and the speed checked per release.

6. Decisions

D3On a host that serves from its CPU, is ONNX Runtime a candidate serving runtime? (a) No: ADR 0019 stands, LiteRT on XNNPACK, or PyTorch where LiteRT loses (its "D2 option (b)"). (b) Yes: ONNX Runtime's CPU provider is timed and held to D7 beside them on such a host. It was the fastest CPU runtime on the Pi (24.4 s against 27.1 s and 28.9 s per served photo) and beat LiteRT on yappy's CPU (ML-7 P4 replay: 38.2 s against 47.8 s); serving holds one runtime, so choosing it is a code change and an amendment to ADR 0019. Open.
D4On a host that serves from an ARM CPU, does DINOv3 serve in fp16? (a) No: fp32, about 24–29 s per photo on the Pi. (b) Yes, in the lnmean_split768 form with the segmenter in fp32, about 14 s per photo. This needs the form in coffeecv/litert_forms.py, justified as a departure from ADR 0022 (constant eps), ADR 0020's fp16 bar extended from yappy's GPU to that host, and R3's checks. It matters only if D1 is (b). Open.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • The models run on the Pi 5 in Docker, on its CPU (LiteRT and PyTorch) and its GPU (LiteRT on Vulkan), timed with ML-7's instrument.
  • ONNX Runtime timed on the Pi's CPU and GPU the same way.
  • Each path's outputs held to D7 against PyTorch on ML-7's 13 photos (ONNX Runtime's GPU path left out: about 10 minutes per photo).
  • The scripts and result records committed in analysis/pi5/.
  • fp16 on the Pi's CPU: why it failed found, a DINOv3 form that runs fast in fp16 found, and held to ADR 0020's fp16 bar on 288 photos.
  • D1 to D4 decided by the owner.

Out of scope

  • An NPU on the Pi. LiteRT's NPU support covers Google Tensor, MediaTek, Qualcomm, Samsung and Intel (OpenVINO) only; the Pi's Hailo and Coral accelerators need their own compilers and int8 or int4 models.
  • int8 models, and fewer than 40 patches per photo: both are model changes that would need their own D7 work.
  • Reporting the XNNPACK fault upstream: the owner decided against it (2026-11-10).
