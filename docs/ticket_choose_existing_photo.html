Ticket OPS-17 · coffee-vision · the web page

The page's Choose Photo lets a visitor on iOS Safari pick a photo they already have, not only take a new one

On an iPhone, Safari's Choose Photo opens the camera, with no way to pick a photo from the library or from Files. The cause is one attribute on the page's file input, capture="environment", there since the page was first written. Written 2026-11-08; D1 and D2 decided: on every phone and tablet the page shows two controls, Choose Photo (an existing photo) and Take Photo (the camera); a desktop keeps one.

1. Summary

Choose Photo is a <label> for a hidden <input type="file"> that carries capture="environment". That attribute asks a mobile browser to open the rear camera directly; iOS Safari then offers the camera only. Without it, iOS shows its own menu (photo library, take a photo, choose a file). The owner's answer (D1, D2): on every mobile browser, iOS and Android, phone and tablet, the page shows two controls, Choose Photo without capture and Take Photo with it; a desktop browser shows Choose Photo only.

2. Background

  • The input (webapp/static/index.html, lines 240 and 251–253 at 89d628a): <label id="photo-label" class="file-btn" for="photo-input">Choose Photo</label>, and the input with a long accept list (JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC/HEIF, AVIF, JPEG XL and the RAW types) and capture="environment".
  • Since when. The attribute came with the page itself (4eebb2c, 2026-08-24); no ticket or ADR chose it, and no test reads it. ADR 0001, "camera first", is about photos against sensor hardware, not about this control.
  • Android, as the owner sees it (2026-11-08): Choose Photo opens the system's menu with two buttons, one for the camera and one for the gallery, so an existing photo can be chosen there today. Desktop browsers ignore the attribute.
  • Other iOS browsers. Chrome and Firefox on iOS run on WebKit, as Safari does, and show the same picker; the page's own browser detection names them CriOS and FxiOS (webapp/app.py). iPadOS Safari reports a desktop Mac user agent by default. Both are D2.
  • The page's own JavaScript reads the input's change event (fileInput, line 273) and disables the control while a photo is in flight (OPS-7 D4). Neither depends on where the file came from.

3. Before and after

Now

  • iOS Safari: Choose Photo opens the camera; a photo already on the phone cannot be sent.
  • Android: the system's menu offers the camera and the gallery (the owner, 2026-11-08).

After

  • iOS Safari: two controls. Choose Photo offers the photo library and Files (and iOS's own camera entry); Take Photo opens the camera in one tap, as Choose Photo does today (D1).
  • Android: the same two controls (D2).
  • Desktop: one control, Choose Photo.
  • A test holds both inputs' attributes and which platforms show Take Photo.

4. What does not change

  • What is accepted. The accept list, the front's 40 MB cap and its canonicalization (ML-19) are as they are.
  • The example photos (OPS-16): their row and click are untouched.
  • Desktop. One control, Choose Photo, as today (desktop browsers ignored capture anyway).

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1No automated test can open iOS Safari's picker. The test can only hold the markup. The owner checks on an iPhone, before and after the deploy.
R2A library photo may reach the front in another form than a camera photo. iOS may send a library HEIC as it is or convert it to JPEG, depending on the accept list; both are accepted and canonicalized (ML-19). To be read from the check service's log on the owner's test uploads, not assumed.

6. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1How does the page offer both an existing photo and the camera? (a) one control, Choose Photo, with no capture: each phone's own menu offers the library, the camera and files (recommended: the smallest change, one button as today) (b) two controls: Choose Photo without capture, and Take Photo with capture="environment", shown on phones only (a one-tap camera kept, at the cost of a second button) (b), on iOS Safari only, 2026-11-08: "D1 answer (b) but only at mobile version of safari […] At Android when I press "choose photo" I see to small buttons from system menu: one is for camera luanching and second one for gallery and photo selection." Choose Photo there loses capture, and a Take Photo control with capture="environment" appears beside it; Android and desktop keep today's one control. Widened by D2 to Android.
D2Which devices and browsers get D1's two controls? (a) every browser on iPhone and iPad: Safari, Chrome and Firefox there share WebKit's picker, so they share the bug; an iPad told apart from a Mac by its touch points (recommended) (b) Safari on iPhone only, as D1 was asked Wider than either, 2026-11-08: "let's make two buttons (choose photo, take photo) for all mobile versions". Every mobile browser, iOS and Android, phone and tablet (an iPad told apart from a Mac by its touch points), shows both controls; a desktop browser shows Choose Photo only.
D3How does the page tell a phone or tablet from a desktop? (a) the user agent (Android, iPhone, iPad, iPod, "Mobi"), and a Mac platform with touch points for iPadOS Safari (b) a coarse pointer (CSS pointer: coarse), which also matches touchscreen laptops (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. An iPhone asking for the desktop site reports a Mac with touch points and gets both controls; a touchscreen Windows laptop and an Android tablet in desktop mode get one, and the tablet's Choose Photo still offers its camera through the system's menu.
D4The two controls' order and look? (a) Choose Photo first, both the same style and width (b) Take Photo a lighter, secondary style (c) Take Photo first (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended: a photo already taken is the common case, and two equal buttons are the easiest to hit.
D5Where is it checked before visitors see it? (a) live: deployed, then the owner checks on an iPhone, an Android phone and a desktop, with --rollback at hand (b) a local app reached from the phone first (a), 2026-11-08. The page is the only change; photo answers are down for the deploy's two minutes or so.
D6What iOS sends from the library, HEIC or a JPEG it converts (R2)? (a) read from the front's check-service log for the owner's test uploads, read-only (b) not looked at (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended; the answer goes into this ticket's Activity.
D7Does the server learn which control a photo came from? (a) no (b) a form field, logged (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended: that is visitor analytics, OPS-3, on hold.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • In webapp/static/index.html, on every mobile browser (D2): Choose Photo without capture, and a Take Photo control with capture="environment" that feeds the same path (the preview, the one-at-a-time lock of OPS-7 D4); elsewhere the page as today.
  • A fast-tier test that holds both controls' attributes and the platform check (D1, D2).
  • Live, verified on the domain (--verify) (D5).
  • On the live site, the owner on an iPhone and on an Android phone: a library photo chosen with Choose Photo and answered, a new photo taken with Take Photo and answered; one control on a desktop (D5).
  • What iOS sent from the library, read from the front's log and written here (D6).

Out of scope

  • Picking several photos at once (multiple): the page answers one photo at a time (OPS-7).
  • The page's look beyond this control.
