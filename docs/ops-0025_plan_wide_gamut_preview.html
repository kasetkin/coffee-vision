Built from a read of the code at 6a28a84, not from the ticket alone, and from P2's run in a browser. Seven things the ticket does not cover turned up. They are folded in below; the seventh needs a decision, Q1 (§2).

  1. The Rec.2020 samples are gone before the preview is made. load_canonical decodes them and keeps only the 8-bit sRGB. It is split in two (decode_canonical, rec2020_to_srgb; load_canonical is still their composition, so training and the store read the same pixels), and the app shrinks the 16-bit samples to the preview's size before it lets go of them. Pillow's own thumbnail on each channel, as 32-bit integers (Pillow cannot reduce 16-bit grey), so the four tabs share one size and one filter: 0.07 s for a 12 MP photo here, on three threads, idle; 0.18 s in the app, beside the model's threads. In line, that would have come before the preview part, so it runs on a thread of its own while the photo is segmented (_shrink_aside), and the previews part waits for it. The cost: the full-size samples (72 MB at 12 MP, 90 MB at the front's 15 MP) live until the shrink ends, a fraction of a second into segmentation, and the shrink shares the CPU with it. P4 holds the host's preview_ms and classification_ms to today's on the smoke photo (P1).
  2. The request line's latency_ms would grow by the third part. It is written when the stream ends. A new classification_ms says when the classification went out, as preview_ms does for the preview; previews_shrink_ms, previews_build_ms and previews_bytes are R4's measurement on every answer (P1).
  3. The front's one-at-a-time gate holds until the stream ends. A visitor who picks a photo right after another's answer waits for the third part too: its encodes and its crossing. R4 is measured on the host in P4; the page's controls stay disabled until the stream ends, as today, and the spinner goes at the classification (P2).
  4. The public API documents two parts. openapi.json, llms.txt and webapp/README.md say the third part comes, during a trial, and that a caller may ignore it (P1).
  5. The smoke's comparison wants exactly two parts. release_probe.py compare-smoke fails any other list, so a release with a third part fails its own smoke until P3 changes it.
  6. No Display P3 profile is in the repo. LittleCMS builds one at import: Display P3's primaries, D65, the sRGB curve as a 256-point table (2,124 bytes, exact against LittleCMS's own sRGB curve on 8-bit samples).
  7. Chromium shows D1's BT.709 transfer darker than the canonical photo reads. In headless Chromium 156 (this container's, --force-color-profile=srgb), on the smoke photo's previews shown bare, the sRGB and P3 JPEGs average (127.7, 119.1, 105.4) and (127.8, 119.1, 105.4), the AVIF (116.4, 108.2, 92.5) and the JPEG XL (117.2, 107.9, 93.2): about 11 levels darker. Decoding the samples with a 2.4 gamma, BT.1886's display curve, predicts (116.2, 105.6, 90.4); the canonical photo's profile, LittleCMS and the models use the inverse of BT.709's camera curve. So in Chrome the two tabs differ from today's in brightness as well as gamut, which is not what the trial compares. Not seen on a phone yet; Safari may differ. Q1.

3. P1 — The third part on the inference host

StatusDone 2026-11-19, in the commit "OPS-25 P1–P3"
Blocked byNone

A new module, coffeecv/preview_formats.py, holds what D1 names: shrink (the 16-bit Rec.2020 samples to the preview's size) and encode_all (the three files). The app starts shrink right after the read-back, on its own thread beside segmentation (finding 1), and calls encode_all after the classification part has gone out; the part is {"part": "previews", "previews": {"p3_jpeg" | "avif10" | "jxl": {"mime", "data"}}}. A failure to make it is logged and the part is left out: the answer is already complete.

  • P3 JPEG: LittleCMS from Rec.2020 to Display P3, relative colorimetric, 8-bit, the profile embedded, quality 85.
  • AVIF: 10-bit, CICP 9/1/9 (Rec.2020 primaries, BT.709 transfer, BT.2020 matrix), quality 80, speed 8 (0.25 s here against speed 6's 0.56 s, for 0.5% more bytes).
  • JPEG XL: jxl_encode.encode_rec2020, the canonical photo's own colour encoding, quality 90 (distance 1.0), effort 7.
  1. Split load_canonical; preview_formats with its tests (sizes, colour tags, colours inside sRGB equal to today's preview, a colour outside sRGB kept). Done 2026-11-19.
  2. The app's third part, its log fields, the release manifest, the docstrings' "the canonical photo as the page shows it" (D7); docs/inference_path.html, the API docs. Done 2026-11-19.
  3. Checks: tests.test_webapp_analyze (three parts, the first two as before; an error part has no third), tests.test_release_manifest. Done 2026-11-19.

4. P2 — The page's tabs

StatusDone 2026-11-19, in the commit "OPS-25 P1–P3"
Blocked byP1

A row of four tabs under the preview: sRGB JPEG (selected), P3 JPEG, AVIF 10-bit, JPEG XL, each with its size in KB, the three new ones "loading" until the third part comes. A tab decodes its image when the part comes; one that will not decode says "can't show" and stays disabled. Choosing a tab swaps #preview's src; every variant has the preview's size, so renderOverlay draws over it unchanged (R5). The examples take the same path, so they get the tabs too (D7). The spinner goes at the classification; the controls stay disabled until the stream ends, since the front's gate holds until then (finding 3). A tab still loading when the stream ends (an error part, or previews that failed on the server) says "not sent", and a stream cut after the classification keeps the answer on the page: before, the stream ended with the classification, so such a cut could not happen.

  1. The tabs, their states, and a static test of them (tests/test_preview_tabs.py). Done 2026-11-19.
  2. Checks: the page in headless Chromium 156 against a saved answer for the smoke photo, driven over the DevTools protocol: the four tabs at 160, 161, 136 and 154 KB, each decoding, today's selected first; on each tab #preview is 768 x 1024 and the overlay's rect the same (R5); the spinner gone and the controls enabled once the stream ended. Finding 7 came from it. On the phones: the owner's, P5. Done 2026-11-19.
