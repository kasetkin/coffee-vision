Ticket OPS-20 · coffee-vision · the web page

The example row and the format chips fold behind buttons, so the photo and its answer are not pushed below the fold

Two blocks sit between the upload controls and the photo, and both stay in full after a pick: the format chips (OPS-18 D5) and the example row (OPS-16 D10). On a phone they take more than a third of the screen, so the visitor's photo and the answer are pushed below the fold. The owner asks for the example row to fold behind an "Examples" button once a photo is picked, and for the format list to open and close with a button of its own. Written 2026-11-08; every decision (D1–D12) taken the same day; not planned, not implemented.

1. Summary

Today the format chips are always shown, and the example row is built once on page load and never hidden again. Every pick therefore puts its photo and answer under both. After this ticket:

  • The chips fold behind a small "Accepted formats" button, which opens and closes the full list.
  • The example row is shown in full only before the first pick. From the first pick on (a photo chosen, taken, or an example clicked) it folds into an "Examples" button. That button opens the row again.
  • Every pick folds both lists, at the moment of the pick. A refusal for a file the page can't read opens the format list again.

The work is one change to the page and its tests, then a release.

2. Background

  • Order on the page. In webapp/static/index.html the card holds, top to bottom: the Choose Photo and Take Photo controls, the format chips (div.formats-hint), #examples, the hidden file inputs, #preview-wrap, the outline toggle, "Use full photo instead", the spinner and progress note. #result comes after the card.
  • The chips. OPS-18 D5 put a chip per accepted format on the page. There are nine, the last being "camera RAW (DNG, CR2, NEF, ARW, …)". tests/test_accepted_formats_named.py reads the spans of div.formats-hint from the HTML and holds them to coffeecv/data/accepted_formats.csv (ADR 0034). The test parses the HTML without running it, so a folded list is still in the HTML and the test still holds. The button must not be a span inside div.formats-hint, or the test would count it as a chip.
  • The format refusal. A file that isn't in an accepted format is refused by the front with a 400, {"error": "could not read this as an image: <reason>"} (webapp/front/check_service.py, the reason from upload_check.refusal). A photo the inference host can't decode is a 400 starting with the same words, so the page can't tell the two apart without matching the reason text.
  • The example row. loadExamples() unhides #examples once the list is read, and nothing hides it again. clearPhoto(), which every pick runs, hides only the preview, the overlay and the result. setBusy() disables the example buttons while a photo is in flight (OPS-16, as OPS-7 D4).
  • The decisions this amends. OPS-16 D10 (2026-11-03) placed the examples "a row under 'Choose Photo'" and said nothing about the row after a pick. OPS-18 D5 named the chips and their order, not whether they are always shown; the chips stay as decided, only folded. ADR 0032 (where the example files come from) and ADR 0034 (which formats are accepted) are not touched.
  • How much it moves. From the screenshot, at 402 px wide the chips take about 80 px (three lines), and the example row with its title about 290 px (four examples on two lines). The preview is capped at 20 rem (#preview max-height). One line of small buttons in their place should move the photo up about 330 px and put the top of the result on the first screen of that phone. P1's check measures it.

3. Before and after

Now

  • The nine format chips are always shown, on three lines on a phone.
  • The example row is shown on load and stays in full through every pick.
  • On a phone, the photo starts below both and the answer is below the fold.

After

  • Under the controls, one line of small buttons: "Accepted formats ▸", and "Examples ▸" after it once a photo has been picked.
  • Each button opens and closes its own list, its arrow turning ▸ to ▾. Both lists may be open at once, the chips above the row.
  • Before any pick, the example row is shown in full, as now. Every pick folds both lists; "Examples" opens the row again.
  • After a pick, the photo starts right under the line of buttons, on every screen.

4. What does not change

  • The accepted formats and their chips. The same nine chips, in the same order and with the same labels (OPS-18 D5), are still in the HTML. Only whether they are shown changes.
  • The examples themselves. The files, examples.json, the captions, the truth line and the live upload on a click (OPS-16 D9, D10, D13) stay as they are.
  • The busy rule. While a photo is in flight, the example buttons are disabled, as now. The two new buttons stay usable, since opening a list sends nothing.
  • A page whose example list cannot be read. It shows neither the row nor the "Examples" button (OPS-16 D13).
  • The front, the public contract and --verify. Nothing outside the page changes; llms.txt and openapi.json keep naming every format.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1A visitor who wants a second example doesn't see how to get one. The row is the only place the examples are offered. Mitigation: the "Examples" button appears on the line right above where the row was, at the moment the row folds.
R2The page jumps under the visitor's eye. If the row folded when the answer arrives, the photo the visitor is looking at would jump up about 290 px. Mitigation: fold it at the pick, before the preview is shown (D2).
R3The new buttons crowd the controls on a narrow phone. At 402 px, Choose Photo and Take Photo already fill their row. Mitigation: the new buttons go on a line of their own, smaller and lighter than the two upload controls (D3).
R4A visitor with an unusual file doesn't know it is accepted. With the list folded, a RAW or HEIC owner sees no chip saying their file works. Mitigation: the button says what is behind it, and a file in a format that isn't accepted is refused with a message, as now. D6 offers a fuller fix.
R5The format test counts the button as a chip. tests/test_accepted_formats_named.py counts every span inside div.formats-hint. Mitigation: the button is a button outside that div. The test then fails loudly if the chips are changed by mistake.

6. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1How is the answer brought above the examples? (amends OPS-16 D10) (a) after a pick, the row folds behind an "Examples" button (b) the row moves below the result for good, so it never sits between the photo and its answer (c) the row stays, and the page scrolls to the answer when it arrives (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. A first-time visitor still sees the examples without scrolling, and a returning one gets the photo and the answer together. (b) puts the examples below the fold before any pick, which is when they are most useful. (c) leaves the order as it is and moves the page without the visitor asking.
D2When does the example row fold? (a) at the pick: a photo chosen or taken, or an example clicked (b) when the answer arrives (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. The preview also sits under the row, so with (b) the photo and the spinner are still pushed down while the visitor waits, and the page jumps when the answer comes in (R2). It also folds on a refusal (too large, unreadable); the button still brings the row back. What a pick is: D10. A pick folds the format list too: D8.
D3Where are the two buttons, and what do they look like? (a) one line of their own under Choose Photo and Take Photo, smaller and lighter than those two, each opening its list right under the line (b) beside Choose Photo and Take Photo, styled like them (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. The two upload controls stay the main actions, and nothing wraps on a narrow phone (R3). Their order on the line: D12.
D4What does an open list do? (a) each button opens and closes its list, its label saying which ("Examples" / "Hide examples") (b) each button opens and closes its list, its label fixed and an arrow turning, ▸ folded and ▾ open, the button's expanded state given to screen readers (c) an open list stays open until the next pick, with no way to fold it by hand (b), 2026-11-08, as recommended. The buttons keep their width, so the line doesn't move when a list opens, and the arrow is the usual sign for something that opens. With (c), a visitor who opened a list to look can't close it without picking. Whether both may be open at once: D11.
D5When is the format list folded? (a) from page load, on every visit (b) like the examples: in full until the first pick, folded after (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended ("d5, d6, d7 all as recommended."). A photo straight from a phone is a JPEG or HEIC, which works without the visitor looking it up. The list matters mostly to someone with an unusual file, who can open it.
D6Does a refusal for an unaccepted format open the list? (a) yes, on any 400 "could not read this as an image" (b) yes, only on the front's format refusal, which then needs a field of its own in the JSON it sends (c) no: the message alone, as now (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. It shows the list to the visitor who needs it, at the moment they need it (R4), using only what the page already receives. A photo that is in an accepted format but won't decode also opens the list, which does no harm. (b) changes the front's answers, the public contract of OPS-18, for the same result. The list opens in its usual place, which after a 400 is right above the refusal's message, the preview being hidden; the chips are not copied into the message (2026-11-08), so the test still holds the only copy.
D7What are the buttons called? (a) "Supported formats" and "Examples" (b) "Formats" and "Examples", shorter (c) "Accepted formats" and "Examples" (c), 2026-11-08, amending (a) of the same day. GLOSSARY.md names these the accepted formats and avoids "supported format", which reads as "a decoder exists"; the page and llms.txt already say any other format is refused. "Formats" alone doesn't say formats of what. Both fit on one line at 402 px, each with D4's arrow.
D8Does a pick fold the format list? (a) yes: every pick folds both lists (b) no: the list stays as the visitor or a refusal left it (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. Otherwise a list opened by hand or by a refusal (D6) puts the next photo under about 80 px of chips. A refusal of that pick opens it again (D6).
D9On which screens do the lists fold? (a) every screen (b) phones and tablets only, by isMobile() (c) below a width, in CSS (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. One behaviour to build, test and explain; on a wide screen the fold costs nothing, the button being where the row was.
D10What is a pick? (a) a file chosen or taken, or an example clicked, the row folding at the click, before the example downloads; a cancelled picker and "Use full photo instead" are not picks (b) as (a), with "Use full photo instead" a pick too (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. A cancelled picker shows nothing new, and "Use full photo instead" keeps the photo, so the lists stay as the visitor left them. Folding at the click puts an example's wait where a visitor's own photo's is.
D11May both lists be open at once? (a) yes: each button folds only its own list, and open lists stack in today's order, the chips above the row (b) no: opening one folds the other (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. Each button does one thing; both open is rare, and the next pick folds them (D8).
D12In which order are the two buttons? (a) "Accepted formats", then "Examples" (b) "Examples", then "Accepted formats" (a), 2026-11-08, as recommended. The buttons are in the order of the lists they open, and the one shown from page load doesn't move sideways when "Examples" appears at the first pick.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • On load, the format chips are folded and an "Accepted formats" button opens and closes them; the nine chips are those of OPS-18 D5.
  • The buttons are on a line of their own under Choose Photo and Take Photo, "Accepted formats" first; each folds and opens only its own list, its arrow and its expanded state saying which, and both lists may be open at once (D3, D4, D11, D12).
  • Before any pick, the example row is shown in full and there is no "Examples" button.
  • After a pick of each kind (Choose Photo, Take Photo, an example), the row is hidden and the "Examples" button is shown. The button opens the row, and picking from the open row folds it again.
  • Every pick folds the format list too, an example's at the click; a cancelled picker and "Use full photo instead" fold neither list (D8, D10). The same on every screen (D9).
  • In headless Chromium at 402×874, once an example's answer is in, the top of #result is inside the first screen. The measured positions are recorded in Activity.
  • When examples.json cannot be read, the page shows neither the row nor the "Examples" button.
  • A 400 "could not read this as an image" opens the format list in its usual place, right above the refusal's message (D6).
  • OPS-16 D10 carries an "Amended by OPS-20 D1" note, and OPS-18 D5 an "Amended by OPS-20 D5" note.
  • tests/test_example_photos.py covers the "Examples" button, and tests/test_accepted_formats_named.py still passes unchanged. scripts/check.sh passes. These three done 2026-11-08 (P1); the release waits for P2. A release is live, --verify passes, and the owner checks it on a phone.

Out of scope

  • Changing the accepted formats, the chips' labels or the public contract (OPS-18, ADR 0034).
  • Changing the examples, their captions or their truth lines (OPS-16).
  • Shrinking the page's header.
  • Recording whether visitors open either list: that is visitor analytics, OPS-3, on hold.
