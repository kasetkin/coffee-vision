Ticket OPS-29 · coffee-vision · logs, timelines and the link between the hosts

The logs measure the photo's crossing and the visitor's upload as they are, and the slow front-to-host link is explained

Reading the logs of ML-37's release showed two timings that do not measure what their names say: the front's crossing_ms ends while most of the photo is still on its way to the host, and the page's upload_ms ends when the answer's headers arrive, after the front has canonicalized. Behind the first is a link that moves 0.25–0.30 MB/s, for 1–16 s of every photo's wait. Written, not planned; the measurements are part of the work.

1. Summary

A photo's wait is the visitor's upload, the front's canonicalization and preview, the photo's crossing to the inference host, and the host's work. The logs time every step but two correctly. The work: (1) measure the link between the hosts and find why it is slow (D2); (2) log the crossing as the time until the host has the whole photo; (3) make the page's attempt times say what they measure; (4) the schema, the reading script and the docs follow.

2. Background

From the logs of release 20261128T1411Z-75d5dd9 on both hosts, 14:10–15:20 UTC on 2026-11-28 (the front's requests.jsonl and nginx access log, the host's app.jsonl and nginx access log), 11 answered photos: the deploy's 5 and 6 from Firefox. Every other timing adds up: the front's total_ms is read_ms + check_ms + canonicalize_ms + crossing_ms + to_end_ms within 15 ms; its nginx's request time is that plus the visitor's upload; the host's nginx's request time is the front's crossing_ms + to_end_ms; the host's latency_ms is decode + crop detection + the mask's build + inference, plus about 0.5 s.

  • The crossing. forward sets crossing_ms when conn.endheaders(body) returns, and counts the answer's to_headers_ms, to_segmentation_ms and to_end_ms from then; its docstring says "crossing_ms is the connection and the whole upload". The call returns when the bytes are in the kernel's send buffer. The host starts its clock (g.t0) only when its nginx has the whole body (proxy_request_buffering on), so the front's to_end_ms exceeds the host's latency_ms by 0.6–5.2 s, growing with the canonical photo's size: the rest of the crossing. Crossing plus that gap: 0.69 MB in 1.5 s, 1.0–1.5 MB in 2.8–5.1 s, 2.0 MB in 7.1–7.5 s, 3.98 MB in 15.7 s.
  • The link. That is 0.23–0.47 MB/s, median 0.30; ping from the front to the host's tailnet address, 75 ms. The rotated logs give the same since OPS-22 (median 0.25–0.31 MB/s on most days from 2026-11-09), except 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 (0.75 and 0.64): it is not ML-37's, and it once was faster. The cause is not known: a tailnet relay (DERP) instead of a direct path, either host's uplink, or TCP's window over 75 ms. On 2026-11-02 (docs/agents/deploy.md) a photo took about 14 s end to end.
  • The page's upload. The page ends an attempt's upload_ms at xhr.upload's load event (onSent in index.html), and timeline_schema.py calls it "until the upload had left the browser". In all six Firefox timelines headers_ms is 0–1 ms: the event fired as the headers came, and the front sends its headers when its stream opens, after canonicalizing. So upload_ms (6.2–15.8 s) held the upload (0.9–3.1 s by the two nginx logs) and the canonicalization (2.9–11.4 s), and to_preview_ms, to_segmentation_ms and to_answer_ms, counted from it, start late. Other browsers are not measured.

3. Before and after

Now

  • The front's crossing_ms: until the bytes were handed to the kernel; the rest of the crossing hides in the answer's times.
  • The page's upload_ms: in Firefox, the upload and the front's canonicalization.
  • The link moves about 0.3 MB/s, for a reason not known.

After

  • A crossing time that ends when the host has the whole photo (D1), the answer's times from then.
  • The page's attempt times named for what the browser can see, the schema's text with them.
  • The link measured, its cause found, and fixed or recorded (D2).

4. What does not change

  • The order of the lines and the work on either host. Only what is timed, and the link.
  • The logs' privacy. No IP, as OPS-3 has it.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1Counts across a change of meaning. A field that keeps its name and changes its meaning splits the record at the release. A new name, or the timeline's version, keeps the old lines readable (timelines.py reads both versions today).
R2The link is the provider's. A slow uplink cannot be fixed here; then the ticket records it, and a smaller photo crossing (the front's settings) is a ticket of its own.

6. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1How is the end of the crossing known? (a) the host logs when it had the body (its nginx's $request_time beside the app's, or a time on the answer's first line) and the reading script joins the two by request ID (b) the front waits for the send buffer to empty (SIOCOUTQ) before it stops the clock (c) only the docs say what crossing_ms is Open.
D2What is measured of the link, and when? (a) tailscale ping and tailscale status (direct or relayed), and a timed copy of a few MB each way, at the start of the work (b) (a), then repeated after any change, and a line in docs/agents/deploy.md Open. The owner: measured when the ticket is implemented (2026-11-28).
D3The page's upload time? (a) renamed for what it is, until the headers, and the schema's text with it (b) measured another way, if a browser gives the upload's end (c) left, the docs explain it Open.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • The link measured per D2: throughput each way, the path (direct or relayed), and the cause of 0.3 MB/s; fixed, or recorded with the reason it stays.
  • The crossing timed per D1, with tests; the reading script shows it.
  • The page's attempt times per D3, the schema's text and its tests with them.
  • One release, and a photo's lines from both hosts and the page read back: the crossing and the host's time adding up to the front's wait.

Out of scope

  • Making the front's canonicalization faster or the canonical photo smaller.
  • The host's own times, which add up.
