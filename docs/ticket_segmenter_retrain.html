1. Summary

Today the segmenter is XL0-v2 (models/seg/xl0_v2.pt). It was fine-tuned on ML-5's 410-photo dataset and the owner's pass-2 labels, and it ships in release 20261009T1740Z-f585d14-allrigs_dino3b16_segxl0_country_s123.   • Augmentation today, and D12. ML-5's fine-tune augments with 4 dihedral views only (identity, both flips, rotation by 180°). Its own code precomputes them in the embedding cache, so each photo is ever seen in only those 4 forms. D12 moves this to Albumentations, generated per sample during training with nothing stored:
    • Library. albumentations 2.0.8 (MIT) is already in pyproject.toml and uv.lock, but no module imports it. Upstream archived it on 2025-07-10. Its successor, AlbumentationsX (2.5.1, 2026-10-11), is AGPL-3.0 or commercial and sends telemetry by default. 2.0.8 checks PyPI for updates on import unless NO_ALBUMENTATIONS_UPDATE=1 is set.
    • Smoke test here, 2026-10-12. It works with OpenCV 5.0.0 and numpy 2.5.3. The mask stays nearest-neighbour, and a warp's exposed border can be set to 255 (ignore). With 9 transforms all on, it costs about 44 ms per 1024 px sample. One seed gives one stream of augmentations.
    • The cost is the encoder, not the library. The cache existed because the frozen XL0 encoder is slow on this CPU. Each photo costs 2.3 s at 4 threads, 1.7–1.8 s at 8 and 1.6–1.7 s at 12 (analysis/ml5_xl0_timing/xl0_timing.py, measured 2026-10-12). On the fly, an epoch of 42 batches of 8 costs about 10–13 min, so 40 epochs take about 7–10 h, against about 1.5 h of caching and 50 min of training today.
    • What it saves. The pass-3 cache would have taken about 7.6 GB on a disk with 11 GB free. The cache was never pushed to the DVC remote (cache: false), so no network transfer is saved.

3. How the segmenter dataset's photos are used

One committed file, labels/ml5/seg_dataset.yaml, lists every photo the segmenter is trained, validated and tested on. coffeecv/seg_dataset.py builds it, and the fine-tune (coffeecv/seg_finetune.py), the review page (coffeecv/seg_review.py) and the OOD guard (coffeecv/fit_ood_probe.py, coffeecv/ood_eval.py) all read their photos from it. ML-6 keeps every rule below and only appends photos. D-, R- and ADR numbers in this section are ML-5's unless they say "this ticket".

3.1 What goes in, and why

SourceWhich photosWhy
Segmenter positiveAll of dataset/segmenter_positives. The owner chose these photos because their setups differ from the pools: framing, container, distance. Most of them don't fill the frame. They are the hard cases: ML-2's segmenter returned an empty mask on 6 of 25 of them (D1, D3).
Pool photoA random draw from the session folders the classifier trains on. The draw takes as many pool photos as there are segmenter positives, shared out in proportion to each folder's size, and skips byte copies of a segmenter positive. Ordinary tray photos are what the segmenter sees most at serving. With an equal count, the hard cases can't crowd them out, and a regression on them shows in their own test line (D12, ML-5 R2).
NegativeAll of dataset/ood_negatives, every batch. The segmenter should return an empty mask on photos without coffee beans, so it trains on them. The OOD guard's probe is fitted on the same negatives (D16).
Internet positiveAll of dataset/ood_positives_internet. These show beans in setups none of the cameras show (D3).

3.2 How the photos are tied into split units and split

  • Split units first. A split unit is a set of photos that always lands in one split. These photos form one (the code calls it a group: seg_dataset.groups, the file's group field):
    • byte-identical files;
    • photos from one source URL;
    • shots chained while each is within 120 s of the last;
    • photos the owner ties together by hand in labels/ml5/split_units.yaml (this ticket's D11).
    A split unit always goes to one split. Why: a copy or a near-identical burst shot on both sides would test the model on a photo it has in effect trained on. 71 segmenter positives are byte copies of other photos in dataset/, 51 of them pool photos (D13, ML-5 R1).
  • Then the split. The file splits 60% train, 15% validation and 25% test within each source, and within each (batch, tag) for negatives. Whole split units move at a time, in an order drawn from seed 511 (params.yaml seg_dataset). Why per source and per (batch, tag): each source and each negative scenario keeps its share in every split, so each gets a test line of its own. Why 25% test: the owner chose 60/15/25 over 70/15/15 (D13), which gives about 32 test photos per positive source. Exception: the 11 green_legume negatives are one 120 s burst, so all of them are in train and none is in test.
  • No mixing across splits. A test photo is never used for training or for choosing a checkpoint, negatives included (D4).
  • Append-only. A listed photo keeps its split forever. A new photo is split on arrival within its source or (batch, tag), and the pool draw tops up to match the new segmenter-positive count. Why: test photos never drift into training, so XL0-v2's test verdicts stay valid evidence beside XL0-v3's (D11).
  • The negatives' own dev/holdout is ignored. The split column in user_realworld.manifest.csv is not used here (D16; this ticket's D1 and R3).

3.3 What each split is for

SplitSegmenterOOD guardWhy
Train Fine-tunes the mask decoder.
  • A positive trains on its owner-accepted mask; dropped photos are left out.
  • A negative trains on an empty mask.
  • Every batch of 8 holds 4 positives and 4 negatives.
  • Each training positive appears once per epoch, in one of 4 views (identity, horizontal flip, vertical flip, rotation by 180°).
  • Negatives are drawn at random with replacement.
  • Half the batches add 1–3 points to the box prompt.
 The probe is fitted on training and validation negatives with a clean tag, so wrong_bean_type is left out. Its bean photos come from the classifier's own test split, minus any in a split unit with a segmenter test photo. A fixed half-and-half batch means more negatives add variety, not weight. The decoder can't drift towards always or never masking as the negative count changes. Flips and rotations are the only augmentation the cached embeddings allow.
Validation Only chooses the epoch, box prompt only. Score = (mean IoU on validation positives + share of validation negatives empty or tiny, under 0.083 of the frame) / 2. Never trains. Fitted on, as train. The checkpoint is chosen without looking at test (D8).
Test Never seen by the fine-tune. The owner judges each test positive's masks blind, two models side by side (D22). Test negatives give the empty-or-tiny rate. ood_eval measures the probe here, and only here (ADR 0016). It is the only evidence no model has seen.

For XL0-v3, D12 changes the Train row's augmentation: the 4 cached views give way to Albumentations transforms drawn per sample. Validation stays unaugmented.

The classifier doesn't use this file. It has its own split over the pools (ADR 0010). A pool photo in the segmenter's test split can be in the classifier's training split (ML-5 R7). A segmenter positive reaches the classifier only as the pool photo it is a copy of.

6. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1Real-world negatives dominate the checkpoint score and the test negatives. They would be 67 of 81 validation negatives and 112 of 153 test negatives. They are mostly far from a tray of beans, so the score may lean on easy cases. D5 decides the score. Reports give the empty-or-tiny rate per batch and tag as well as overall.
R2The positive side barely changes, so the paired test can show no difference there. There are 11 new labels on top of about 200, and 71 test positives. XL0-v1 against XL0-v2 already gave McNemar p = 1. The case for XL0-v3 rests on negatives, and the positive comparison only guards against a regression.
R3Manifest holdout photos end up in training. 119 of them would, under D1 (a). Anything that reads the manifest's holdout as unseen would be wrong once a model trains on them. D1 decides.
R4Disk. The new cache needs about 7.5 GB. 24 GB was free on 2026-10-10, but only 11 GB on 2026-10-12 (plan Q1). The pass-1 cache (3.5 GB, cache: false, rebuildable) can go first, after the owner's OK. Under D12 there is no pass-3 cache, so this risk is gone.
R5The 6 new class_005 pool photos never reach the segmenter. The pool draw tops up by count and did not pick them. They reach the classifier only through a re-crop (D8). This is accepted by design (ML-5 D12), and recorded here so nobody expects otherwise.
R6One scene shot on two phones lands in both train and test. The 2026-10-11 hopper: the iPhone shot goes to train, the two OnePlus shots 28–30 s later go to test, because their clocks are never compared (§2), so they form two split units. The angle and framing differ, but the beans are the same. The same can happen whenever the owner shoots one scene with a phone that records a UTC offset and one that does not. D11 decides.
R7The fine-tune takes 7–10 h. Under D12 the frozen encoder runs on every sample in every epoch (§2). The run goes in the background with a log, and a toy-scale run proves the path first. A rerun after a fault costs the whole run again, so the run should start only when the host can be left to it.
R8The comparison cannot say what helped. XL0-v3 differs from XL0-v2 in its data and in its augmentation (D4, D12). The paired test says which model is better on test, not why. Separating the two would take a third model, and that is not planned.
