1. Summary

The page's preview is the inference host's JPEG thumbnail of the 8-bit sRGB read-back. It is untagged, so every browser shows it as sRGB, with colours outside sRGB clipped. The canonical photo it comes from is Rec.2020 at 16 bits, so it holds colours that a Display P3 screen can show and the preview drops. After this ticket:

  • The trial. The inference host makes three wide-gamut previews from the canonical photo's Rec.2020 samples (D1) and sends them as a third part of the answer, after the classification (D5). The page shows a row of tabs under the preview to flip between them and today's (D2). The owner looks at them on a Pixel 9 Pro and an iPhone (D4).
  • Adoption. The chosen format becomes the preview, for visitors' photos and the examples alike; the tabs and the third part go (D6).
  • Gamut only (D3). The canonical photo is standard range since ML-29 (ADR 0041), so no format made from it can be brighter than white. Keeping the light above white is a change to canonicalization: ML-34.

2. Background

From analysis/ml29/research_browser_hdr_preview.md (2026-11-18), which cites a primary source for each claim and lists the ones it could not confirm, and from the code and earlier tickets:

  • The preview today. <img id="preview"> (webapp/static/index.html) gets data:image/jpeg from _preview_part (webapp/app.py). That thumbnail is at most 1024 px, quality 85, with no ICC profile, made from load_canonical's 8-bit sRGB. Its docstring promises "the pixels the model sees" (OPS-7 D8). The examples' preview is the same thumbnail. The bean overlay is two 2D canvases in the default sRGB.
  • The canonical photo. Rec.2020 primaries, BT.709 transfer, 16-bit lossy JPEG XL, standard range (ADR 0033, ML-19 D7). A gain map is applied and its light above white tone-mapped back (ADR 0041, ML-29 D33). Its 16 bits are finer steps, not headroom.
  • How much lies outside sRGB. Measured in ML-19 (analysis/ml19/README.md, gamut.py): of 1,152 Display-P3-tagged photos, 0.86% of pixels fall outside sRGB and 0.29% by more than 2%. 120 photos have more than 1% outside, 18 more than 10%: mostly user_realworld negatives and two label cards; the highest bean photo, from the Pixel, 57.8%. The owner has seen such photos look different in sRGB and P3.
  • iPhone 15 Pro, 16 Pro, 17 Pro, Safari. WebKit colour-matches tagged images, and the panels are "Wide color (P3)". Safari decodes AVIF (16+) and JPEG XL (17+).
  • Pixel 9 Pro, Chrome. Chrome colour-manages tagged images and decodes AVIF. Whether it sees a wide-gamut screen in the Pixel's default "Adaptive" colour setting is unconfirmed. JPEG XL on by default from Chrome 155 (2026-10-06), by news reports, not a primary source; the tab on the phone confirms it.
  • Firefox (not one of the owner's test browsers). JPEG XL on by default from Firefox 158 (2026-10-13); 152–157 and ESR 153 have it built in but off. By news reports (Betanews, Slashdot), and the owner's own reading, 2026-11-19.
  • No room for a second worker. The inference host has about 3.6 GB free beside the live worker, which costs about 3.3 GB (ADR 0026, OPS-10), so a test release beside the live one is not possible.
  • The encoders. imagecodecs 2026.10.10, the version canonical.py pins, has avif_encode (bit depth and CICP primaries, transfer and matrix) and the JPEG XL encoder.

3. Before and after

Now

  • The preview is an untagged 8-bit sRGB JPEG, read as sRGB by every browser.
  • Colours outside sRGB are clipped before the preview is made.
  • The answer has two parts: the preview, then the classification.

After the trial release

  • Under the preview, tabs: sRGB JPEG (today) · P3 JPEG · AVIF 10-bit · JPEG XL, each with its size; today's selected.
  • The answer has a third part, previews, after the classification.
  • A tab whose format the browser cannot show says so.
  • The bean overlay lines up with whichever tab is shown.

After adoption

  • One wide-gamut preview, the owner's choice, with today's JPEG as the fallback where a browser cannot show it; no tabs, two parts again.

4. What does not change

  • What the models read. 8-bit sRGB from the canonical photo (ADR 0033). The preview is display only (GLOSSARY.md, "Preview").
  • The canonical photo and canonicalization, at the front and in the store (D3).
  • The one streamed request (ADR 0023): the previews are a part of the same answer, and the server keeps no photo between requests.
  • What a visitor sees first. The preview part and the classification part arrive as they do today; the third part comes after both (D5).
  • The overlay's canvases, in sRGB (D7).

D1The wide-gamut preview's file? (a) JPEG with a Display P3 ICC profile, 8-bit (b) PNG with cICP (c) 10-bit AVIF (d) JPEG XL (a), (c) and (d), all three tried, 2026-11-19: "ideally I want to test all variants". D4What decides adoption? (a) the owner's look at the tabs on the Pixel 9 Pro and an iPhone (b) plus a new measurement of pixels outside sRGB (a), 2026-11-19; R1 is already answered.
D5When are the extra previews sent? (a) a third part after the classification (b) in the preview part (c) only when the visitor turns a switch on before uploading (a), on every answer during the trial, 2026-11-19. D6What ships at adoption? — 2026-11-19: the tabs and the third part go; the chosen format is the preview, with today's sRGB JPEG as the fallback where a browser cannot show it. Which fallback, if any, is settled with the choice.
D7The overlay, the examples and the preview's promise? — 2026-11-19: the canvases stay sRGB; the examples get the same previews, which come for free since theirs is the same thumbnail; _preview_part's "the pixels the model sees" becomes "the canonical photo as the page shows it", and GLOSSARY.md defines Preview.
D8The AVIF's and the JPEG XL's transfer? (the plan's Q1) (a) BT.709, as D1 wrote it (b) the sRGB curve (b), 2026-11-19: "Use sRGB transfer for both files, so I will see difference only in gamut." Chromium shows BT.709-tagged files with a display gamma, about 11 levels darker than the JPEGs (the plan's finding 7). Amends D1: both stay Rec.2020; the AVIF is CICP 9/13/9, the JPEG XL 16-bit Rec.2020 with the sRGB curve, no longer the canonical photo's own encoding.
D9A label on each preview? (a) none (b) the format's name burnt into each file's corner (c) a label on the page (b), 2026-11-19, the owner's idea, as recommended: each file names its format (the tab's words) in its bottom-right corner, white outlined in black, 4% of the long side, so a screenshot says which file the browser showed; pure white and black are the same grey in every space, so the label adds no gamut difference.
D10Today's sRGB preview labelled too? (a) yes, every visitor's and the examples' first picture carries "sRGB JPEG" during the trial (b) only the three new files (a), 2026-11-19, as recommended: the four tabs alike. The label goes with the tabs at adoption (D6).

7. Scope and acceptance

  • The share of pixels outside sRGB is known (R1: ML-19's measurement).
  • D1–D7 settled, HDR brightness split off to ML-34.
  • The three variants' encode time and the third part's size are measured on the inference host before the release.
  • The live page shows the four tabs, today's selected, for visitors' photos and the examples; the overlay lines up on each; --verify checks the third part.
  • The owner has looked at the tabs on a Pixel 9 Pro and on an iPhone, and R2's browser facts are recorded as confirmed or not.
  • The owner's choice is recorded. If one is adopted, it ships as D6 says; if none, the tabs go and the idea goes to .out-of-scope/ with what was seen.

Out of scope

  • Any change to what the models read, or to canonicalization.
  • HDR brightness: ML-34.
  • HEIC as a preview format: only Safari shows it.
  • A wide-gamut overlay.
