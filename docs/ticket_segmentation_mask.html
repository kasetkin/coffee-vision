Ticket ML-2 · coffee-vision · classification pipeline

Bean segmentation stage: mask with EfficientViT-SAM-L0, crop to the beans, then classify

Add a stage before classification. A segmentation model marks the bean region: the part of the photo covered by a pile of coffee beans. The mask is applied to the photo, and the photo is cropped to a rectangle that keeps 95% of the bean region. Patches are then cut from that rectangle and classified by the DINOv3 classifier as today. The model is EfficientViT-SAM-L0, used first as pretrained and then fine-tuned on our photos, with one output class: the bean region, coffee beans only (D10). The stage runs identically in training and serving. This ticket records the design, the owner's decisions, and what it costs. Terms follow GLOSSARY.md. Nothing is implemented yet.

1. Summary

Today the bean region is found by hand-built heuristics: texture to find the tray, then saturation to separate beans from the rim (crop_tray.py), with per-session settings in dataset/<session>.crop.yaml for training and live tray localization at serving (infer.crop_to_bean_region). The heuristic has a known failure (warm reflections on the tray's inner wall read as beans) and one open residual (oneplus, ~16%). It also needs hand-tuning for every new setup. This ticket replaces it with one learned model that runs the same way at both ends.

The segmenter only decides where the beans are. What the classifier sees changes in two ways: the crop boundary moves, and non-bean pixels inside the crop are filled. So the DINOv3 head and the OOD probe must be refitted on patches from the new stage. That is cheap, since the backbone is frozen.

The classification metric will not show much of a gain (R1). The stage is judged on its own mask and crop metrics and on classification non-inferiority. Its value is robustness on new setups and the removal of per-session crop tuning. It is not a higher val score.

Training codeThe repo's script is for full distillation on SA-1B across 8 GPUs. There is no fine-tuning recipe for a small custom dataset, so we write our own loop (P5).

Prior art in this repo

coffeecv/crop_tray_sam.py is an unused MobileSAM prototype (via ultralytics) that replaced only the heuristic's stage 2. Its finding carries over directly: a single point prompt at the tray center snaps to one bean, because every bean is its own closed contour. A 3×3 grid of foreground points gives the whole pile. Any prompt we choose must be tested against this failure (D5).

Scale of the data

9 crop sessions, 938 cropped photos in the four cam_* pools (406 / 220 / 220 / 92). Raw photos are under dataset/<session>/ and are DVC-tracked. There are no bean masks yet.

3. Pipeline, before and after

Now

  1. Decode photo (load_rgb_image).
  2. Heuristic bean region: tray by texture, beans by saturation, largest all-bean rectangle + safety trim. Training uses per-session crop.yaml.
  3. Bean pitch by FFT.
  4. 40 bean-unit patches → frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 + linear head (C=0.1).
  5. Mean of patch probabilities; OOD probe on embeddings (threshold 0.5).

After

  1. Decode photo.
  2. Segment: EfficientViT-SAM-L0 at 512 with a fixed prompt → bean-region mask, upsampled to full resolution.
  3. Mask + crop: fill non-bean pixels (D3), crop to the rectangle that keeps ≥95% of the bean region (D4). If the mask is empty or tiny, skip this step and use the whole original photo, unfilled (D18).
  4. Bean pitch by FFT on the filled crop; the estimator itself is unchanged (D19).
  5. 40 random patches inside the crop; a patch with less than 80% bean region is rejected and redrawn, and after a capped number of attempts the best rejected patches top up to 40 (D17). No check on fallback photos (D18).
  6. DINOv3 + refitted head; refitted OOD probe.

Steps 2–3 are one function, called by the training crop stage and by infer.py. This follows the train/inference parity rule: the same estimator runs on both sides, with no accurate-offline, cheap-live split. The function logs mask_area_frac, bean_frac_in_crop and retained_frac per photo, the same way beans_across is logged today. Patch sampling also logs whether the photo fell back to the whole frame (D18) and how many of its patches are below the 80% bar (D17).

4. What does not change

  • The classifier recipe. Same frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16, cls_mean features, linear head with C fixed at 0.1 (ML-1 D3), 40 patches, TTA off. Only the head's and the probe's weights are refitted.
  • Bean-unit patch sizing and the FFT pitch estimator. They run on the new, filled crop as they run on the old one. P2 checks that the pitch does not shift (D19). Only patch placement gains a check: the 80% bean-region rule (D17).
  • The evaluation protocol from ML-1. Pooled per-class split, val patch macro-F1 selects, test patch macro-F1 reported, paired multi-seed adoption, no camera reported separately.
  • Raw data and class labels. Masks are a new, separate DVC-tracked input; judge verdicts and the owner's review decisions are committed to git (D16). Photos are not edited.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The classification metric cannot show the benefit. Val patch macro-F1 is ~0.975 and nearly flat across choices (ML-1 R1). The current crop is already an all-bean rectangle, so in-distribution patches are mostly beans either way. The gate is non-inferiority on val/test plus the mask and crop metrics in §7. The real gain is on photos unlike the training set, which no current split measures.
R2Filled pixels change the embeddings. The OOD probe's 0.5 threshold was calibrated on unmasked patches. Refit the head and the probe on new-stage patches, then rerun the OOD holdout against today's bar (0/22 genuine refused, 56/56 negatives caught). Patches that straddle a fill boundary are a new kind of input; bean_frac_in_crop shows how often this happens.
R3A pretrained SAM repeats the heuristic's failure by taking reflections on the tray wall as beans, or snaps to single beans. This is why a fine-tuning phase exists. P2 measures it before any integration work.
R4Latency on the CPU server. /classify takes 7.0 s idle and 12.5 s during a sweep, and training shares the VM. No gate (D8). L0 at 512 is measured on the VM in P0 and again at P6, and the added seconds are reported in the ship decision. The segmenter runs on the PyTorch runtime at both ends (R5).
R5 Owner decision, 2026-09-30. triton: patch the vendored copy so it is not needed. RMSNorm's Triton kernel is imported only when used; L0 on CPU never uses it. onnx: not installed. The segmenter runs on the PyTorch runtime in training and serving, so nothing uses it; its import in apps/utils/export.py is moved inside the ONNX export function, the only place that needs it (owner, 2026-09-30). Each patch to the vendored copy is listed in a note beside it (third_party/efficientvit/PATCHES.md). Any further missing imports found in P0 are handled the same way: patch if unused on CPU, install if useful.
R6No human ground truth (D6). Labels come from the pretrained model itself, corrected where the judge flags them. The eval metric is the judge's pass rate, not IoU. A blind spot that the judge and SAM share would go unseen in both training and eval. Planted defects measure the judge's miss rate (P1), and every eval number is reported next to it. Because planted defects only test errors someone thought of, the owner also reviews ~50 of the judge's real P2 verdicts blind, and the agreement rate is reported (D12). Fine-tuning can only improve on what the judge catches, so its gain is bounded by the judge's quality. The eval photo list is fixed before any fine-tuning and is never trained on. The judge sees full-resolution tiles along the mask boundary as well as the downscaled overview, following the rule that crops and masks are verified at full resolution, not in thumbnails.
R6bThe judge is an external, paid, non-deterministic service. A pinned model ID and a committed prompt. Verdicts are committed to git, keyed by photo and mask hash, and dvc repro never calls the judge, so a rerun is a choice, not a surprise (D16). Cost is measured on a 20-photo pilot before any full pass, for both claude -p and the API (D15). Overlays are re-encoded from pixels, so photo metadata (GPS, C2PA) is never sent. The machine that runs it needs either a Claude Code login or an API key.
R7Every cropped pool regenerates. New DVC hashes everywhere, and a large push. Expected, not a defect. Keep crop_tray.py's outputs reproducible until P6 so the comparison in P4 stays paired. Check that the DVC remote is back from maintenance before P3.
R8Mask resolution. A mask made at 512 px on a ~4000 px photo is ~8× coarser at its edges. Harmless for a 95% crop rule. It would matter only for per-bean work, which is out of scope.

6. Decisions (answered 2026-09-30)

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1Does the new stage replace the tray heuristic or run after it? (a) Replace crop_tray at both ends; the segmenter sees the whole photo. (b) Keep stage 1 (rough tray box by texture) and segment only inside it, as the MobileSAM prototype did. (a), as recommended. One learned step instead of two, and no per-session crop.yaml. crop_tray.py stays in the repo until P6 as the comparison baseline; deleting it is a later cleanup. ADR 0011.
D2What exactly is "bean" in the mask? (a) The bean region: the pile, including shadows and gaps between touching beans; tray, rim, table and hands excluded. (b) Individual bean pixels only. (a), as recommended. Patch texture stays as the classifier knows it. Separate piles in one photo are allowed. This definition goes into the judge's prompt.
D3What fills non-bean pixels inside the crop? (a) ImageNet mean RGB, which normalizes to about 0 (rounded to uint8 as (124, 116, 104), the channels come out at +0.006, −0.005 and +0.008; corrected at P3). (b) Black. (c) No fill: the mask only chooses the rectangle. (a), as recommended, with no comparison arm: P4 runs gray fill only.
D4The exact crop rule. (a) Quantile trim. (b) Bounding box of the largest connected blob. (c) Blob, then quantile trim. (d) Largest inscribed all-bean rectangle, as today. (a), as recommended. Cut each side at the 1.25% / 98.75% quantile of the bean pixels' x and y coordinates. The four cut strips hold at most 5% of bean pixels together, so ≥95% is kept by construction. The keep fraction is one param in params.yaml; retained_frac and bean_frac_in_crop are logged per photo.
D5How is the model prompted? (a) A fixed box covering the whole image. (b) A fixed grid of points. (c) Automatic "segment everything" mode. (a), as recommended, in both phases. If the pretrained model returns single beans with (a), the P2 baseline falls back to (b). The judge's corrective points (D6) are used only to make training labels; at serving time the prompt stays the fixed box. Owner decided 2026-10-01, after P0 showed no fixed prompt finds the pile on tray photos: keep (a), the whole-image box, and keep the widest multimask output (multi3). Both are in params.yaml as seg_prompt and seg_mask_select. On frame-filling photos multi3 ≈ the whole frame, which is right. On tray photos (362 of 938) the mask is expected to be the table, paper or tray, so the P2 baseline measures that. The judge-corrected labels (D6) and fine-tuning (D7) are the route to tray masks.
D6Where do the masks come from, and who says they are right? Proposed: hand-corrected masks (~60+20 eval, ~150 train). Owner decided, differently: no hand annotation. The pretrained EfficientViT-SAM draws every mask and Claude judges it.
Role: pass/fail plus corrective point prompts on failures ("add here", "exclude the rim here"). SAM is rerun with those points and judged again, for at most 2 rounds; a mask still failing is dropped. This way the hard cases (reflections, rims) enter the fine-tuning set instead of being filtered out.
Mechanism: a committed script calling the Anthropic API, with a pinned model ID and prompt, and verdicts in a DVC-tracked file. Provisional: the owner will revisit this. Superseded by D15 (model and mechanism) and D16 (storage).
Trust: planted defects. Known-good masks are corrupted automatically (shift, erode, dilate over the rim, drop a corner) and mixed in blind with the real ones; the catch rate is the judge's measured quality. The known-good masks are base masks the owner accepted (D12); the bar is D14.
Sets: fine-tuning photos from the classifier's train split only; eval photos from val/test plus the OOD negatives (expected: empty mask). The lists live in one committed file. Negatives: dev only, split for the segmenter (D11). ADR 0011.
D7What does fine-tuning train? (a) Mask decoder only, with image-encoder embeddings cached. (b) Encoder and decoder. (c) Decoder + adapters/LoRA. (a) first, as recommended. Loss: focal + dice, as in SAM. Go to (b) only if (a) misses the P5 gate.
D8Latency budget for the new stage. +1.0 s, +1.5 s, +3.0 s, or no hard limit. No hard limit. Measured in P0 and P6 and reported in the ship decision; P0 is no longer a kill switch.
D9What happens when the mask is empty or tiny? (a) Fall back to the whole frame, flag it, and let the OOD probe decide. (b) Refuse as "no beans found". (a), as recommended. mask_area_frac is logged on real traffic; a gate comes only once it can be calibrated.

Design review (answered 2026-09-30)

A question-by-question review of what D1–D9 left open. The terms used here (coffee bean, bean region, negative, judge, base mask, planted defect, catch rate, false-fail rate) are defined in GLOSSARY.md.

IDQuestionOwner decision
D10Does the mask mark only coffee, or any pile of bean-like things? Coffee beans only, if the segmenter can be shown to do it (D11's negative test). A coffee bean is the seed without cherry pulp or parchment, green or roasted, whole or split. A bean region is a pile of mostly whole coffee beans, including stray foreign objects lying inside it. Piles of other seeds, grains, legumes, ground coffee, cherries or mostly broken beans get an empty mask. A pretrained SAM has no notion of classes and will outline any pile, so only fine-tuning (P5) can deliver this. If P5's negative test fails, the segmenter ships as "any pile" and the OOD probe alone decides whether it is coffee.
D11Which negatives does the segmenter use, and what counts as success? OOD dev negatives only, split by scenario tag into seg-train (~2/3) and seg-eval (~1/3). Near-duplicate shots stay on the same side. The OOD holdout is never used by the segmenter. Their labels are empty masks by definition, with no judge involved. Negative test (added to the P5 gate): ≥ 90% of pile-like seg-eval negatives produce an empty or tiny mask, reported per tag, with user_samerig (green legumes, confusable grains on our own tray) on its own line and the interval shown. Pass rates are reported per roast state and are not gated. If only the negative test fails with decoder-only training, try encoder + decoder (D7 (b)) once before giving up on coffee-only.
D12Where do the known-good base masks come from, and how does the owner review? The owner accepts or declines prebuilt pretrained masks; no drawing. Base masks are drawn only from val/test, spread across sessions and cameras, green and roasted, and never used for training. About 50 are needed (25 for prompt development + 25 held out for the final check), so ~60–70 photos are reviewed. The owner also reviews ~50 randomly drawn real P2 masks blind (the judge's verdict is shown only after the click), and the agreement is reported next to the catch rate. Tool: a local review server in the devcontainer, reached through VS Code's port forwarding. Each click is written straight to a decisions file committed to git. The screen shows the photo with a thin mask outline (Space toggles it), click-to-zoom at full resolution, the resulting crop box dashed, Accept/Decline buttons (A/D keys), optional one-click decline reasons (rim/tray included, beans missed, snapped to single beans, not coffee, other), and a progress counter. Every photo is accepted, declined or unreviewed; clicking again overwrites. The tool shows any committed list of (photo, mask) items and is reused for P2's failure review and judge audits.
D13What makes a mask acceptable? One rule, used word for word on the review page and in the judge's prompt:
Accept the mask only if all of these hold: (1) nothing inside the outline is tray, rim, table, hand or reflection, apart from a few pixels along the edge; (2) no part of a coffee-bean pile bigger than about one bean lies outside the outline; (3) the outline covers each pile as a whole, including the gaps, shadows and anything lying inside it, and doesn't trace individual beans; (4) if the photo has no pile of mostly whole coffee beans (green or roasted; not other seeds, grains, legumes, ground coffee, cherries, parchment or mostly broken beans), the mask is empty or covers at most a few beans. Otherwise, decline.
D14When is the judge good enough? Catch ≥ 90% of consequential planted defects and false-fail ≤ 10% on unmodified base masks, per defect type. A defect is consequential if it drops the D4 crop's IoU with the clean crop to ≤ 0.90, or adds non-bean area equal to ≥ 5% of the bean region. Smaller defects are also planted; their catch rate is reported, not gated. The prompt is developed on the first 25 bases, with at most 3 revisions. The final check runs once, on freshly generated defects on the 25 held-out bases. If it fails, work stops and the owner decides.
Owner confirmed 2026-09-30, after the blind pilot audit: the 5% line stays, and a judge stricter than the owner's eye is fine. The owner had accepted a consequential dilate (a uniform tray band adding 13% of the pile's area) that Opus declined. Owner decided 2026-10-01, after the final check failed on dilate (41/48 caught, gate 90%): accept the judge as is (Opus 5.5, prompt aacc4dda51ee). Known weakness: it can miss an even tray band adding about 6–9% of the bean area. Every other type was caught at ≥ 98%, false fails were 4/50, and the owner had accepted a 13% band in the pilot audit.
D15Which model is the judge, and how does it run? Chosen after the P1 20-photo pilot. The pilot runs Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 on the same planted-defect set; the cheaper model that meets D14 is pinned. It runs either as headless claude -p --model … from a committed script (on the owner's Claude Code plan) or as a script on the Anthropic API with Message Batches. The pilot reports measured cost for both, projected to the full run; the owner picks. Nothing runs beyond the pilot until then.
Owner decided 2026-09-30: Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5) via headless claude -p on the owner's Claude Code plan. Pilot (20 items from base_dev, prompt aacc4dda51ee, claude -p): Opus caught 10/10 consequential and 5/5 small defects with 0/5 false fails; Sonnet 5.5 caught 9/10 and 4/5 with 1/5 false fails. Opus costs about $0.082 per item at API-equivalent prices, Sonnet $0.041; on the plan it is usage, not money. n = 2 per type, so the pilot compares the models; it does not show that D14 is met. The API route is implemented but was not measured (no anthropic package or key). ADR 0011.
D16How are judge outputs stored? Judging is an annotation step, not a DVC stage: dvc repro never calls the judge, and a rerun is a deliberate command. The final label masks are stored and DVC-tracked; the verdicts and corrective points are kept as the record of how each mask was made. Verdicts, corrective points and the owner's review decisions are small text files committed to git, so they don't depend on the DVC remote. Each verdict is keyed by photo and mask hash. A stage that computes pass rates reads only stored verdicts and fails loudly, naming the masks to judge, if one is missing.
D17Where are patches placed? Crop first (D4), then random patches inside the crop. A patch whose bean-region share is below a params.yaml value (default 0.80) is rejected and redrawn, the same way in training and serving. After a capped number of attempts (a param, e.g. 20 × 40), the best rejected patches, highest bean share first, top the photo up to 40. The number of below-threshold patches is logged per photo.
D18What counts as a tiny mask, and what happens then? A mask_area_frac threshold in params.yaml, set in P2 at half the smallest bean-region area on a genuine eval photo. An empty or tiny mask means patches are drawn from the whole original photo, unfilled, and all of them are accepted (D9 (a)). Every trigger is logged.
Owner set 2026-10-01: seg_min_area_frac = 0.083, half the smallest owner-accepted base mask (0.1657). The plan's version of the rule (half the smallest judge-accepted pretrained mask) gives 0.45, because only frame-filling pretrained masks pass, and would send every correct tray mask to the fallback. At 0.083 no pretrained eval mask, OOD positive or negative triggers it.
D19What does the FFT pitch estimator see? The filled crop: mask, fill and crop first, then the FFT. _K_LO is not touched. P2 compares beans_across for the old and new crops of the same photos. It passes if the median relative change is ≤ 5% and 95% of photos change by ≤ 15%. If it fails, the pitch is estimated on the largest all-bean window instead. The check looks for a systematic shift; the data already varies in distance and lens.
Owner changed 2026-10-01, after P2: the gate is the median only, on beans_across (not pitch in px, which shifts about −2.8% just because the D4 crop is smaller). The tail is reported, not gated, next to a trim-only control: the old crop cut by the D4 crop's per-side share (1.25%), with no mask or fill. On P2 the median is 0.08% (pass), and p95 is 24.9% against the control's 17.4%, so most of the tail is the estimator jumping between FFT bins, not the fill. The all-bean-window fallback is dropped: on these photos the crops are already all beans.
D20When do the new photos (dataset_new_ignored/) join? After ML-2. ML-2 runs on today's data, which is green coffee apart from 126 roasted photos (class_010 in all three 2026-08-30 sessions; the ticket first said 46, pixel only, corrected 2026-09-30). The data-merge ticket (classes 011/012 and the rest) re-judges the new photos, fine-tunes the segmenter again, and refits the head and probe. The segmenter's eval list only ever grows: no ML-2 eval photo moves to training, and the post-merge model is also scored on the ML-2 subset. There is no roasted guard in ML-2; P4/P5 choose what ships, and a new release follows the merge.
