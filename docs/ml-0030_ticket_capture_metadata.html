1. Summary

Stub. The question is whether anything a camera records about the shot, beside the picture, gives either model a better input: the size of a bean in pixels from the subject distance and focal length, or the scene's brightness from the exposure. Nothing is proposed yet.

2. Background

Counted 2026-11-14 over the 2,851 photos in dataset/ (analysis/ml29/README.md); to be checked when the ticket is written.

  • How many. Subject distance in 1,687 photos; 35 mm-equivalent focal length in 2,181; exposure time, f-number and ISO in about 2,655; brightness value in 2,124. How trustworthy a phone's subject distance is has not been looked at.
  • Today. A canonical photo holds no metadata but its colour encoding (ML-19 D21), and the front never forwards or stores the original upload (ADR 0013). Anything used would have to be read before that, at intake and on the front, and carried beside the canonical photo.
  • Why it might matter. EXPERIMENTS_LOG.md's cross-rig test of 2026-08-09 found the models' transfer between cameras tracking how far the bean scale was extrapolated. That protocol was retired (.out-of-scope/cross-camera-evaluation.md), so this is a lead, not a measured need.
