Ticket ML-11 · coffee-vision · segmenter labelling page

Let the owner paint a mask on the labelling page: erase stray parts and fill small holes with a round brush

On the labelling page a declined mask can be corrected only through the model (green and red points) and a fence that cuts it to a rectangle. Neither removes a small blob outside the beans that a rectangle cannot exclude, nor fills a small hole inside a correct mask. The owner asks for a brush: hold e and drag to erase, hold w and drag to fill, with a see-through round cursor and a radius from 1 to 100 pixels set on the right panel. Owner request, 2026-10-26. The review tool's second purpose is to help the segmenter produce masks for the positives before training, with three ways to improve a bad mask: green dots, red dots and a bounding box. Add a "painter" mode: if the owner presses e and moves the mouse with the left button pressed, the mask under it is erased. The cursor stays visible but shows what is underneath: a round circle about 10 pixels across, with a thin border and a see-through inside. It is for erasing small parts of the mask outside the bean region. An inverted mode, w with the same gestures, fills small holes inside a correct mask. A numeric field on the right control panel sets the brush radius, from 1 to 100 pixels.

1. Summary

Today every mask on the labelling page (python -m coffeecv.seg_review label) is the model's output, at most cut to a fence. After this ticket the owner can also paint on it: an erase brush (hold e, drag) and a fill brush (hold w, drag), radius set in a number field on the right panel. Strokes are recorded like points and fences, as data the server applies to the mask at full resolution, so every label stays reproducible from its index row. The work splits into: the recorded strokes and their application on the server; the page's brush, cursor, live preview and radius field; and the label's provenance (the ADR 0015 amendment and the point probe).

2. Background

  • The correction flow (coffeecv/seg_review.py docstring and LabelSession.redraw): the owner declines a mask (D or 1–5), places points (hold g or r and click) or a fence (hold b and drag), and Enter redraws: the redraw model draws the whole-photo box plus every point so far through multi3, then fence_mask cuts the result to the fence. The result is the next round's mask, judged again; a session gives each photo seven rounds (ADR 0030). Points and the fence carry forward from round to round; U undoes the last point or fence.
  • What the page shows: a server-rendered JPEG, the photo at VIEW_LONG_SIDE = 1600 px with the mask outlined in magenta (review_masks.view_image), and a 1024 px full-resolution tile around a clicked spot shown at 512 px (zoom_tile). The browser never holds the mask itself, so painting with live feedback needs the mask on the page.
  • Scale: the photos are phone photos larger than the 1600 px view, so one screen pixel of the view is a few photo pixels. A radius of 1 photo pixel is under one screen pixel; a radius of 100 screen pixels is a few hundred photo pixels. D1: the page counts screen pixels, the record photo pixels.
  • Records: each round's index.csv row holds the points (include, exclude) and fence that drew its mask; the verdict rows copy them, and labels.csv carries the accepted label's fence. A redraw with nothing new since the shown mask is refused (400).
  • Provenance: ADR 0015 has every label drawn by the model from the owner's points, amended by ML-9 D7 for the fence, which bounds the mask after the model. The point probe (coffeecv/seg_point_probe.py) uses accepted round-2+ labels as references for "does a model follow points", and skips fenced ones because no model redraws them from their points. A painted label is the same case.
  • Keys in use: A, D, 1–5, g, r, b (held), U, Backspace, Enter, Space, N, arrows. E and W are free. The digits 1–5 decline the mask, so typing a radius into the field must not reach the page's key handler.

3. Before and after

Now

  • Three corrections: include points, exclude points, a fence.
  • A stray blob outside the beans, inside any useful rectangle, stays unless the model drops it.
  • A small hole inside the beans stays unless the model fills it.
  • The page shows the mask only as an outline baked into a server JPEG.
  • Every label is the model's output, perhaps fenced.

After

  • Two more: hold e and drag to erase, hold w and drag to fill.
  • The cursor over the photo is a circle of the brush's size, a thin border with a see-through inside, coloured by mode.
  • A number field on the right panel sets the radius, 1–100 pixels of the screen (D1); each stroke is recorded with its radius in photo pixels, scaled from the view's size on the screen at the time.
  • Each stroke shows on the page as it is drawn, a see-through tint over the photo and its outline: light green where w fills, light red where e erases (D7); U undoes the last stroke, as it does a point.
  • Enter sends the strokes with the points and fence; the server draws the mask, fences it, then paints it, and records the strokes in the index row, the verdicts and labels.csv.
  • A painted label is marked as one and is no reference for the point probe.

4. What does not change

  • The owner judges every mask (ADR 0015): a painted mask is judged like any redraw before it becomes a label.
  • The rounds (ADR 0030): a painted mask is a round like a redraw (D4).
  • Points and the fence work as they do; painting is applied after them.
  • The blind paired mode (PAIRED_PAGE): it judges models' masks, so it gets no brush.
  • Serving and fine-tuning: a painted label trains a fine-tune like any accepted label; nothing in serving changes.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The tint shows where the brush went, not the edge that results. Until Enter the outline is the shown mask's (D7), and the tint is drawn at view resolution where the server paints at full resolution, with the radius scaled to photo pixels (D1). Both apply the same geometry (a disc of the radius along the stroke), and after Enter the page shows the server's mask, which is the one judged. A test checks that the server's painted pixels are those within the radius of the stroke.
R2Typing a radius could decline the mask. Digits 1–5 decline it. The key handler ignores keys typed into the field; Enter there applies the radius, not a redraw.
R3A painted label could pass for the model's. The point probe would then score a model against pixels no model drew. Strokes are a recorded column, and the probe skips a painted label as it skips a fenced one.
R4Strokes carried to a later redraw may land on a mask that moved. A stroke made on round 3's mask is applied to round 4's model output if strokes carry forward (D3). Erase outside the beans and fill inside them stay right whatever the model draws, but a stroke along an edge may not. D3 decides; the preview shows the result before the owner presses Enter.
R5A long stroke makes a long index row. Mouse-move events are dense. Stroke points are kept only where they move at least a pixel at view scale, and rounded like points, to four decimals.

6. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1The radius 1–100 counts which pixels? (a) Photo pixels: the brush paints the same area of the photo whatever the window size, and the record means the same on every screen; on the 1600 px view a radius of 1–2 is under a screen pixel, and the cursor is drawn at least a few pixels wide. (b) Screen pixels of the view: what the owner sees is what is set, and the record converts to photo pixels at that view's scale. Both, 2026-10-26: "web interface should show radius as pixel on current screen. But path that we save to the disk should remember them as photo pixels, so web tool should scale tool radius correctly." The field and the cursor are in screen pixels; each stroke's path (fractions of the photo, as points) and radius (photo pixels) are converted at the scale the view had when the stroke was drawn.
D2Where can the owner paint? (a) On the main view only. (b) Also on the full-resolution zoom tile on the right, where a small hole is four times larger than on the view. (a), 2026-10-26: only the main view.
D3Do strokes carry to later rounds, as points and the fence do? (a) Yes: every stroke so far is applied after each redraw, and U undoes them. (b) No: strokes apply to the mask they were drawn on; a later redraw with new points starts from the model's mask again. (a), 2026-10-26, as recommended.
D4When can the owner paint, and what does Enter do with strokes alone? (a) As with points: after a decline (or on an unjudged redraw); Enter with only new strokes reruns the same pipeline (decoder, fence, strokes) into the next round's mask, judged again. (b) Strokes on the mask under judgement without declining it, accepted with it. (a), 2026-10-26, as recommended: the same as points.
D5Is e (and w) held, like g, r and b, or a toggle? (a) Held: hold e and drag; release ends the mode. (b) Toggle: press e once to enter erase mode, again (or Esc) to leave. (a), 2026-10-26: held down.
D6A painted label changes what ADR 0015 says a label is. Amend it? (a) Amend ADR 0015, as ML-9 D7 did for the fence: a label is the model's mask, fenced, then painted by the owner; a painted label is no reference for the point probe. (b) Keep painting out of labels. (a), 2026-10-26: amend. The amendment is written when the brush works (plan P2): ADR 0015, amended 2026-10-27.
D7What shows the strokes before Enter? The outline is drawn into the server's JPEG, so it cannot follow the brush (plan Q1). (a) The page draws the edited mask's outline itself, updated as the stroke is drawn. (b) A see-through tint over the painted area, on top of the server's outline. (b), 2026-10-26: "let's use seethrough colors. Light green for adding mode, when 'w' is held. And seethrough light red for removing mode, when 'e' is held." The outline changes at Enter.
D8The radius field's starting value (plan Q3). (a) 5 screen pixels, the last value set kept in the browser from photo to photo. (b) A fixed start on every photo. (a), 2026-10-26, as recommended.
D9Undoing a stroke carried from an earlier round: the shown mask already has it painted in, and the page cannot show the pixels under it (plan Q2). (a) Keep the undo; the page says "undone; Enter redraws to show it". (b) A second route serving the mask before strokes, at a decoder run per page load or a second stored mask per round. (a), 2026-10-26, as recommended.
D10After Enter, do the strokes still show? The page reloads with the new mask, and D7's tint showed only the strokes not yet sent (P3). (a) Only the strokes not yet sent; the earlier ones are in the mask. (b) Every stroke so far, those that drew the shown mask too, until U undoes one. (b), 2026-10-27, the owner trying the brush: "I want to see them so I can verify new mask and see Undo change anything." An undone earlier stroke loses its tint at once; the mask changes at Enter (D9).

7. Scope and acceptance

  • Holding e (w) and dragging with the left button over the photo erases (fills) a disc of the set radius along the path; the stroke shows as it is drawn, tinted see-through light red (light green) over the photo (D7).
  • The cursor over the photo, while e or w is held, is a circle of the brush's size with a thin border and a see-through inside, in one colour for erase and another for fill.
  • A number field on the right panel sets the radius, 1–100 screen pixels, clamped; the stroke is recorded with that radius in photo pixels (D1); typing in it triggers no page key.
  • U and Backspace undo the last stroke, point or fence, in the order made.
  • Enter sends the strokes; the server applies them after the model and the fence, at full resolution, and refuses a redraw with nothing new, strokes counted.
  • Strokes (mode, radius, path) are recorded in the round's index.csv row, its verdict rows and labels.csv; rows written before have none and read as no strokes.
  • The point probe skips painted labels.
  • ADR 0015 amended (D6), and the seg_review.py docstring and the page's key list describe the brush.
  • Tests in tests/test_seg_review.py: erase and fill paint the right pixels, after the fence; strokes are recorded and carried to later rounds (D3); an old row reads as no strokes; a strokes-only redraw is accepted and an unchanged one refused.

Out of scope

  • Painting in the blind paired mode.
  • Brush shapes other than a disc, and keyboard shortcuts for the radius.
  • Painting on the zoom tile (D2).
  • Re-labelling past sessions with the brush; it is for sessions from the next one on.
