Ticket ML-37 · coffee-vision · what both models read

The canonical photo stores linear Rec.2020 light in 16-bit integers, and one read-back to 8-bit sRGB feeds both models and the preview

1. Summary

Today the canonical photo stores standard-range Rec.2020 in 16-bit integers with the BT.709 curve. Light above white, from a gain map or an HDR main image, is tone-mapped away when the photo is canonicalized. Every reader rounds the photo to 8-bit sRGB first, and the resizes after that round again. After this ticket:

  • Conversion 1, original → canonical. Everything up to the encode stays: the colour declaration honoured, the gain map applied, LittleCMS into Rec.2020, BT.2408's EETF when the picture's peak is above white, clipped to [0, 1]. The photo is then stored as linear Rec.2020 in 16-bit integers, intensity target 203, in a lossy JPEG XL at today's settings. The store and the front do the same (D1).
  • Conversion 2, canonical → models. One read-back serves the segmenter and the classifier, in training and in serving: the uint16 samples → 3×3 matrix to linear sRGB → clip to [0, 1] → sRGB curve → rounded to 8 bits once (D2, D3). Crops stay lossless 8-bit JPEG XL and patches uint8 (D10). Whether the resizes after the read-back round again is D11.
  • Conversion 3, canonical → preview. The preview is a JPEG of that same 8-bit picture: the visitor sees what the models read. OPS-25's wide-gamut trial ends without a format adopted (D4). The front makes the picture from its own canonical photo with the same read-back and sends it to the browser at once, about 5 s before the host could; the host's segmentation line carries the mask and the box (D15, D24).

Every canonical photo in the store changes, so the store is re-canonicalized, the pool re-cropped and the classifier refitted, and the result is compared under a bar fixed before the runs (D13). Research expects the models' input to move only by the lossy coding (§2, R1).

2. Background

Read from the code on 617c944 and from the tickets named; the research items are dated.

  • The jxlinfo report, checked. Both lines are the design, not a fault. "Transfer function: 709" is ML-19 D7: "Rec.2020 primaries, D65, Rec.2020's own transfer curve (BT.709's)". Both settings write it (jxl_encode.encode_rec2020, JXL_TRANSFER_FUNCTION_709). canonical.header_problems refuses any other, so the inference host accepts nothing else. "Primaries: Rec.2100" is libjxl's name for the enumeration (BT2100), whose primaries are Rec.2020's. The curve is a storage curve: the read-back undoes it exactly through the committed profile coffeecv/data/rec2020_bt709.icc (ML-19 D46), so the models' input is right. But a viewer shown the file reads BT.709 with a display gamma: Chromium drew such files about 11 levels darker (OPS-25 D8). A linear transfer has one reading. This ticket replaces the curve with linear light (D1).
  • Conversion 1 today (canonical.decode, to_rec2020, encode). The photo is decoded and its colour declaration honoured (ADR 0040). A gain map is applied (ADR 0041). LittleCMS converts the colour, float in the store and integer on the front for most uploads (ADR 0036). When the picture's peak is above white, the light is rolled off in linear Rec.2020 by BT.2408's EETF at that peak (ML-29 D33), then clipped to [0, 1] and rounded to uint16 with the BT.709 curve. The encode is lossy JPEG XL, 16-bit integer: quality 95, effort 7 in the store, quality 90, effort 3 on the front.
  • Conversion 2 today. load_canonical = decode_canonical (uint16) then rec2020_to_srgb (LittleCMS, no optimization, to uint8 sRGB). Every model reads that, in training (segcrop_session, canonical_store) and in serving (webapp/app.py). After it, the path stays 8-bit:
    • The segmenter's resize, model_io.sam_resize, uses PIL bilinear on uint8.
    • The stored crops are lossless 8-bit sRGB JPEG XL (ML-19 D32, crop_io).
    • The classifier's patches are kept as uint8 at patch_store_size 448 (dataset.py), augmented by albumentations on uint8, and resized by PIL (eval_resize) before / 255.
    • Serving cuts its patches the same way (infer.py).
    Both models take float32 at their input; the serving graphs read float32 (coffeecv/litert.py), DINOv3 computing in fp16 inside on yappy's GPU (ADR 0019).
  • Conversion 3 today. _preview_part makes an untagged sRGB JPEG at quality 85, at most 1024 px, from the same uint8 array the models read. OPS-25's trial is still live (20261119T1649Z-bf6e9df). It adds three wide-gamut previews (P3 JPEG, 10-bit AVIF, JPEG XL) as a third part of the answer, with format tabs on the page, and OPS-25 closed with no format chosen. Its trial was to be ended "in another ticket": this one.
  • Why sRGB stays the models' input.
    • ML-19 D2 chose sRGB "for now".
    • ML-29 P10: gain-map crops against today's, paired over three seeds, test −0.0009 (−0.0032 to +0.0031). D16's bar was not met, and the owner adopted gain maps on other grounds.
    • ML-34's experiment B, on unaugmented heads: PU21 in place of sRGB scored −0.004 to −0.008 on test. The HDR light alone gave +0.002, inside the seed range.
    • The literature: linear light into an sRGB-pretrained network scored 5 to 19 AP lower (research_hdr_pu21.md).
  • Why the preview is not wide gamut. In OPS-25, Chrome 157, Firefox 160 and DuckDuckGo on the Pixel 9 Pro all draw pages in sRGB, so every tab looked the same (Chromium issue 492734022, Mozilla bug 1879568, both open). Only Safari on the iPhone showed the wide-gamut tabs.
  • ML-34, superseded. Its aim, a canonical JPEG XL that a browser shows with HDR brightness, is dropped (D4, D5). Its open decisions are answered here:
    • Its D1, how the light above white is kept: it is not. It is tone-mapped at canonicalization, as today (D1, 2026-11-25). The 2026-11-23 answer, linear half-float keeping it, was revised.
    • Its D3, the store or the front only: both (D1).
    • Its D4, whether the models read the light above white: no, an sRGB read-back (D2), as its research recommended.
    • Its D2, whether the models' input stays exactly ML-29's: no. Every photo is re-canonicalized and the classifier refitted (D13); the input moves by the lossy coding only.
    Its measurements carry over. Experiment A: in the median gain-map photo, 1.5% of bean pixels lie above the EETF's knee and 0.3% above white, the iPhones most.
  • The pinned encoder can write a linear canonical photo. On 2026-11-23, on a synthetic ramp: imagecodecs 2026.10.10's jpegxl_encode, given float16 samples and primaries=BT2100, writes a lossy 16-bit float codestream with the linear transfer. On 2026-11-25 (research_canonical_int_vs_half.md §2): imagecodecs and the repo's strip encoder jxl_encode.encode_rec2020 (ML-19 D52), set to the linear transfer, write byte-identical uint16 files; the header reads 16-bit integer, BT2100, linear, D65. Only the strip encoder can set the intensity target (one field); imagecodecs's files declare libjxl's default, 255.
  • How many bits the classifier's input needs, researched 2026-11-23 at the owner's request (research_bits_classifier.md): 8-, 10-, 12-bit and float16 sRGB against float32, each rounded once at the crop with the resizes after it in float. Going above 8 bits gives no measurable gain.
    • DINOv3's feature moves by a median 1 − cosine of 3.5e-7 at 8 bits, 2.2e-8 at 10, 1.5e-9 at 12 and 3.8e-9 at float16, on 1,600 test patches of 266 photos. For scale: the fp16 cast serving applies, 3.8e-8; the store's own lossy coding, 1.1e-5; a 1-pixel shift of the patch, 6.6e-4. From 10 bits up the input is as close to float as serving's own cast.
    • The live head (exp290) gives the same answer in every arm on all 1,600 patches and 266 photos: test macro-F1 0.9554 and the same F1 in all 10 classes.
    • Head refitted on seeds 42/123/7, 8-bit against float16: macro-F1 −0.0007, +0.0000, −0.0006, mean −0.0004, photo-bootstrap 95% CI [−0.0015, +0.0006], inside D13's proposed bar; the lowest class, Colombia, −0.0024. Unaugmented heads (the 8 views would have taken about 14 hours); 10 and 12 bits not refitted.
    • Where the rounding sits matters more than how many bits: today's path rounds three times (crop, 448 px patch, 224 px input), moves the feature 130× more than one 8-bit rounding at the crop, and is the only bit-depth arm that changes answers (4 patches, 2 photos; macro-F1 0.9559). The 8-bit effect is largest on boxes under 300 px, which are upsampled, but stays below a 1-pixel shift.
    • At the pixel, 8-bit rounding (0.29 levels RMS) is below the store's coding error (1.03) but not below the noise in the flattest bean regions (0.15); the downscale to the model's input averages it away (0.033 against the coding's 0.20).
    All arms use today's canonical photo. A fine-tune on float input was not measured: 2,621 of the dataset's 2,920 originals are JPEGs, the coding error exceeds 8-bit rounding, and the augmentation is far coarser than an 8-bit step, so no gain is expected.
  • How many bits the segmenter's input needs, researched 2026-11-23/24 the same way (research_bits_segmenter.md, numbers in bits_seg_report.txt): the whole photo rounded once where load_canonical rounds today, the resize to the encoder's 1,024 px in float. The live segmenter is the XL0 encoder with the xl0_v7r_s123 decoder: the owner's request names XL1, which is downloaded but not used (params.yaml, the decoder's card). Going above 8 bits gives no measurable gain.
    • The encoder's embedding moves by a median 1 − cosine of 7.9e-7 at 8 bits, 3.5e-8 at 10, 2.0e-9 at 12 and 3.7e-9 at float16, on 625 photos. For scale: an fp16 cast of the input, 3.2e-8; a second q95 JPEG XL generation, 8.6e-6; a 1-pixel shift, 3.8e-4. From 10 bits up the input is as close to float as an fp16 cast.
    • Masks against the float32 arm's, 360 test photos: median IoU 0.99999 at 8 bits (lowest 0.998 on a served mask) and 1.00000 from 10 bits up; no mask became empty and no photo changed between mask and fallback in any arm. Crop boxes moved on 9 of 237 positives at 8 bits (at most 4 px), on none from 10 bits up except one at float16 (1 px).
    • Against the pass17 labels (validation, 177 positives and 88 negatives, seg_finetune's own measures): mean IoU 0.96990 in float32, float16, 12 bits and today's path, 0.96989 at 10 bits and 0.96992 at 8 bits; paired change 8-bit against float32 +1.6e-5, photo-bootstrap 95% CI [−6.9e-6, +5.1e-5]. Every negative stays empty in every arm. The test split has no labels (the owner reviews it blind).
    • As for the classifier, where the rounding sits matters more than how many bits: today's path rounds twice (the photo, then the 1,024 px input) and moves the embedding 30× more than one 8-bit rounding, and its masks most (test: lowest IoU 0.990 on a served mask, boxes moved on 30 of 237 positives; validation: one box by 114 px). It still scores the same against the labels.
    Reduced: 360 of 472 test photos within the time budget; no fine-tune. All arms use today's canonical photo.
  • Linear 16-bit integer against half-float, researched 2026-11-25 at the owner's request (research_canonical_int_vs_half.md), on D8's 20 photos at half scale, both settings, the read-back rounded once to 8 bits:
    • Linear uint16 compresses exactly as well as half-float on the same tone-mapped picture: file 1.000× per photo (0.999–1.001), pixel error 1.854 levels RMS in the store and 2.770 on the front for both, the same block-mean error and the same share of changed 8-bit values. libjxl turns integer and float input into the same float picture before XYB.
    • No worse in the shadows: dark pixels 1.836 against 1.836 levels RMS (store), 2.490 against 2.488 (front). uint16's own steps near black are coarser before coding (0.156 against 0.107), but 12–16× below the coding error there.
    • Neither can be told from today's BT.709 file at the same intensity target. The target is the one change that shows: 203 against 255 makes files 4.5% (store) and 6.1% (front) smaller and the block-mean error 6% and 9% higher, far inside D27. Keeping light above white in half-float would cost 4.4–5.5% on gain-map photos.
    • Encode, decode and memory alike within 4–8% (4 full-size photos). DINOv3's feature, uint16 against half-float: median 1 − cosine 8.1e-6, below the store's own coding change.
  • What the front should send to yappy, researched 2026-11-25 at the owner's request (research_front_forms.md): 40 originals across formats and cameras, the front's settings (q90, e3, the 15 MP cap), 2 cores pinned, median of 3 runs, on a workstation, not the front. Mean ± standard deviation:

3. Before and after

Now

  • The canonical photo is Rec.2020 with the BT.709 curve, 16-bit integer, standard range, intensity target 255. Viewers draw the curve differently.
  • Every reader rounds it to 8-bit sRGB, and the resizes after that round again: three roundings to the classifier, two to the segmenter.
  • The preview is a JPEG of that 8-bit sRGB. OPS-25's trial adds three wide-gamut previews and format tabs.
  • LittleCMS does every colour step, both ways.

After

  • The canonical photo is linear Rec.2020, 16-bit integer, standard range, intensity target 203, lossy JPEG XL. One reading in any viewer.
  • One read-back (matrix, clip, sRGB curve) rounds to 8-bit sRGB once and feeds both models. Crops and patches stay 8-bit. The resizes after it are D11's.
  • The preview is a JPEG of that picture: the visitor sees what the models read. No tabs. The front makes the picture and sends it first; the host sends the mask and box (D15).
  • LittleCMS still converts originals into Rec.2020, on float in the store and on the front alike (D12). The read-back is numpy (D9).

4. What does not change

  • The models read standard-range 8-bit sRGB. ML-19 D2; the research in §2 found nothing above 8 bits for either model. Neither model's architecture or weights change; the classifier's head and the OOD probe are refitted on the new input.
  • One canonical photo per photo, JPEG XL, Rec.2020 primaries, 16-bit integers, made at the front (ADR 0033). Its transfer and intensity target change; its role does not.
  • What is read from an original, and the tone map. Colour declarations are honoured or refused (ADR 0040), gain maps are applied (ADR 0041) and light above white is rolled off by ML-29 D33's EETF before the encode, as today.
  • The crops and patches. Lossless 8-bit sRGB JPEG XL crops (ML-19 D32), uint8 patches, augmentation on uint8 (D10).
  • The two settings. The store's and the front's quality, effort and caps stay (ADR 0036). The front's colour path is D12's.
  • The format list, the one streamed request (its preview part split in two, D15, D24), the masks' geometry (ADR 0034, ADR 0023).

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The models' input moves more than the change of form explains. Before any retrain, each form's model picture, today's and the new, is compared with the uncoded float reference on every dataset photo: mean and largest level difference on 32×32 block means (ML-29 D27's measure), per camera, with the gain-map photos apart, and the work stops for the owner where the new form breaks D27 and today's does not (D20). Expected: the lossy coding only, since the tone map is unchanged. On 20 photos the linear uint16 file reads back like today's at the same intensity target (§2); the move to 203 adds 6–9% to the block-mean error.
R2Lossy JPEG XL keeps less detail from linear integers than from today's BT.709 integers. Measured 2026-11-25 (§2): no. Same size and error at the same intensity target, shadows included. The intensity target (D8) acts as a quality offset alike for every form. Re-checked at full scale in R1's comparison and on the file sizes at both settings.
R3The front loses precision, time or memory. The front's integer path uses LittleCMS's optimized 8-bit matrix-shaper, which into a linear uint16 output quantizes light to 1/16384, about 0.2 sRGB levels a step near black, up to 0.8 levels before the encode (D12's research). Gain maps already take the front to 392–487 MB and 12–15 s on the largest photos, against its 600M (ML-29 R6). D12 (a) takes the float path, which the research found faster on gain-map photos and about 2.4 s slower on plain ones, worst 10.6 s and 433 MB; measured on ML-29 R6's photos before the release.
R4The inference host's read-back is slower or larger. Small after 2026-11-25: uint16 in, uint8 out, as today, with no float copy of the photo kept and no peak pass. D9's numpy read-back took 0.89 s at 15 MP against today's 1.85 s (research, on this machine). Measured against ML-21's decode time.
R5Float resizes and augmentations do not match today's 8-bit ones. Narrowed 2026-11-25: the augmentation stays on uint8 patches (D3, D10), so albucore's internal 8-bit rounding and CoarseDropout's fill units (D11's research) no longer apply. What remains is D11 (b): a float sam_resize and eval_resize change the segmenter's masks and DINOv3's input slightly (within 2.02 levels of today's on 13 photos), judged in R1 and D13.
R6An old canonical photo meets a new host, or the reverse. The header check is the gate: it accepts only the linear transfer and intensity target 203. The front and the host ship in one release, and the example photos are re-made in it. A canonical photo sent by a tool from before the release is refused, naming its encoding.
R7A reader of the canonical photo still assumes the BT.709 curve. The committed profile rec2020_bt709.icc and every caller of decode_canonical and load_canonical (the review pages, review_masks, make_example_photos, the bean-pitch estimate) are listed in the plan and moved to the one read-back. A tool that skips it would read linear light as if it had the curve: a much darker picture, caught by a test on a known photo.
R8The new crops do not fit on this machine's disk. Closed 2026-11-25 by D10: the crops stay 8-bit, so the re-crop replaces today's 13.2 GB pool with one of the same size. The 2026-11-23 research had found +85 to +150 GB for 16-bit crops against 62 GB free.
R9Float patches and photos do not fit in memory. Closed 2026-11-25 by D3 and D10: patches and the segmenter's training photos stay uint8 (3.61 GB and 2.6 GB). The 2026-11-23 research had found float16 patches peaking at about 8.1 GB against about 7 GB available.
R10Making the preview's picture at the front (D15) breaks its memory limit or delays the crossing. The read-back on a whole 17 MP photo peaks at 1,074 MB more; in strips of 256 rows, 101 MB, identical output (§2). The read-back is written in strips, and the front's peak is measured on ML-29 R6's photos with R3. Its 0.7 s runs while the photo crosses the link, not before it.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • ADR 0033 and the ML-19 decisions D1 and D9 amend (D7, D31, D46) amended, ADR 0036 for D12, and two new ADRs (D19): the linear canonical photo and its read-back, and the front drawing the preview (amending ADR 0023 and ADR 0031).
  • The canonical photo written as D1 says by both settings, the store's and the front's, and refused in any other form by the host. Shown by jxlinfo (linear transfer, Rec.2100 primaries, 16-bit integer, lossy, intensity target 203) and by tests.
  • The read-back as D2 and D3 say, rounding to 8 bits once, the only way any reader opens a canonical photo; rec2020_bt709.icc retired (R7).
  • R1's comparison on every dataset photo, per camera, before any retrain, against the uncoded reference under D20's stop, and the file sizes at both settings.
  • The front's and the host's time and memory on ML-29 R6's photos (R3, R4).
  • The store re-canonicalized and the pool re-cropped through their DVC stages, and pushed to the DVC remote.
  • The classifier's head and the OOD probe refitted, and the paired three-seed comparison judged against D13's bar; on a miss, D21's second run. The owner's decision.
  • The preview is the models' input picture (D4). OPS-25's tabs, third part and preview_formats.py removed. GLOSSARY.md's "Model picture", "Preview" and "Canonicalization" say so.
  • The front sends the preview line (jpeg, D24) before the photo crosses to the host, made from the same model picture the host's read-back makes (a test on a known photo, and D22's check on the live hosts); the host's segmentation line carries the mask and box; the page shows the preview first and the mask when it comes, the preview alone when the host is offline, and the answer without a preview when the front could not make one (D15, D23). The front's time and peak memory with it measured on ML-29 R6's photos (R10).
  • The logs follow the new lines (D23, D25–D27): the front's and the host's log lines, the timeline at version 2, the reading script's steps, timeline_check.py and the logging docs; one request's three lines read back for an answer, no preview, and a refusal after the preview.
  • Adopted: one release of store, front, host and models, the examples re-made, verified on the live site. Not adopted: the reasons recorded here and in .out-of-scope/.

Out of scope

  • A preview with wide gamut or HDR brightness (OPS-25, ML-34). The canonical photo keeps wide gamut but not the light above white, so an HDR preview would need a new canonical form.
  • Model input above 8 bits, light above white, PU21 or any other HDR encoding as the models' input (§2; ML-34 D4, experiment B).
  • A fine-tune on float input (§2: not expected to gain).
  • Changes to what is read from an original: colour declarations, gain-map forms, second images (ML-29).
  • New models or a change of backbone; capture metadata (ML-30).
