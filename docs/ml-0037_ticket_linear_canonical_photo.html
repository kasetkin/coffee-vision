Ticket ML-37 · coffee-vision · what both models read

The canonical photo stores linear Rec.2020 light in half-float, and one float read-back to sRGB feeds both models and the preview

The owner's review of the wide-gamut and HDR work after ML-29, OPS-25 and ML-34: both models read best from standard-range sRGB, and few phone browsers draw a wide-gamut preview correctly. So the canonical photo stores the light itself (linear Rec.2020, 1.0 = reference white, 16-bit half-float, lossy JPEG XL). One float read-back makes the models' input, with no 8-bit step, and the preview is made from that same picture. This ticket supersedes ML-34 and continues ML-29. The owner's decisions D1–D5 are recorded; D6–D14 are open. Not planned, not implemented.

1. Summary

Today the canonical photo stores standard-range Rec.2020 in 16-bit integers with the BT.709 curve. Light above white, from a gain map or an HDR main image, is tone-mapped away when the photo is canonicalized. Every reader rounds the photo to 8-bit sRGB before anything else. After this ticket:

  • Conversion 1, original → canonical. The canonical photo stores linear Rec.2020 light as 16-bit half-float in a lossy JPEG XL, where 1.0 is reference (diffuse) white. Light above white is kept: applied gain maps, PQ and HLG main images, and a float JPEG XL's values above 1.0. The store and the front do the same (D1).
  • Conversion 2, canonical → models. One read-back serves the segmenter and the classifier, in training and in serving. It decodes the half-float samples, applies a 3×3 matrix to linear sRGB, tone-maps or clips to [0, 1] and applies the sRGB curve, all in float32. Nothing on the path is 8-bit: not the read-back, the resizes, the stored crops, the patches or the augmentation (D2, D3).
  • Conversion 3, canonical → preview. The preview is the read-back's picture, the one the models read, encoded once for display. OPS-25's wide-gamut trial ends without a format adopted (D4).

Every canonical photo in the store changes, so the store is re-canonicalized, the pool re-cropped and the classifier refitted, and the result is compared under a bar fixed before the runs (D13).

2. Background

Read from the code on 617c944 and from the tickets named.

  • The jxlinfo report, checked. Both lines are the design, not a fault. "Transfer function: 709" is ML-19 D7: "Rec.2020 primaries, D65, Rec.2020's own transfer curve (BT.709's)". Both settings write it (jxl_encode.encode_rec2020, JXL_TRANSFER_FUNCTION_709). canonical.header_problems refuses any other, so the inference host accepts nothing else. "Primaries: Rec.2100" is libjxl's name for the enumeration (BT2100), whose primaries are Rec.2020's. The curve is a storage curve: the read-back undoes it exactly through the committed profile coffeecv/data/rec2020_bt709.icc (ML-19 D46), so the models' input is right. A browser shown the file directly reads BT.709 with a display gamma: Chromium drew such files about 11 levels darker (OPS-25 D8). This ticket replaces the curve with linear light (D1).
  • Conversion 1 today (canonical.decode, to_rec2020, encode). The photo is decoded and its colour declaration honoured (ADR 0040). A gain map is applied (ADR 0041). LittleCMS converts the colour, float in the store and integer on the front for most uploads (ADR 0036). When the picture's peak is above white, the light is rolled off in linear Rec.2020 by BT.2408's EETF at that peak (ML-29 D33), then clipped to [0, 1] and rounded to uint16 with the BT.709 curve. The encode is lossy JPEG XL, 16-bit integer: quality 95, effort 7 in the store, quality 90, effort 3 on the front.
  • Conversion 2 today. load_canonical = decode_canonical (uint16) then rec2020_to_srgb (LittleCMS, no optimization, to uint8 sRGB). Every model reads that, in training (segcrop_session, canonical_store) and in serving (webapp/app.py). After it, the path stays 8-bit:
    • The segmenter's resize, model_io.sam_resize, uses PIL bilinear on uint8.
    • The stored crops are lossless 8-bit sRGB JPEG XL (ML-19 D32, crop_io).
    • The classifier's patches are kept as uint8 at patch_store_size 448 (dataset.py), augmented by albumentations on uint8, and resized by PIL (eval_resize) before / 255.
    • Serving cuts its patches the same way (infer.py).
    Both models take float32 at their input; the serving graphs read float32 (coffeecv/litert.py), DINOv3 computing in fp16 inside on yappy's GPU (ADR 0019).
  • Conversion 3 today. _preview_part makes an untagged sRGB JPEG at quality 85, at most 1024 px, from the same uint8 array the models read. OPS-25's trial is still live (20261119T1649Z-bf6e9df). It adds three wide-gamut previews (P3 JPEG, 10-bit AVIF, JPEG XL) as a third part of the answer, with format tabs on the page, and OPS-25 closed with no format chosen. Its trial was to be ended "in another ticket": this one.
  • Why sRGB stays the models' input.
    • ML-19 D2 chose sRGB "for now".
    • ML-29 P10: gain-map crops against today's, paired over three seeds, test −0.0009 (−0.0032 to +0.0031). D16's bar was not met, and the owner adopted gain maps on other grounds.
    • ML-34's experiment B, on unaugmented heads: PU21 in place of sRGB scored −0.004 to −0.008 on test. The HDR light alone gave +0.002, inside the seed range.
    • The literature: linear light into an sRGB-pretrained network scored 5 to 19 AP lower (research_hdr_pu21.md).
  • Why the preview is not wide gamut. In OPS-25, Chrome 157, Firefox 160 and DuckDuckGo on the Pixel 9 Pro all draw pages in sRGB, so every tab looked the same (Chromium issue 492734022, Mozilla bug 1879568, both open). Only Safari on the iPhone showed the wide-gamut tabs.
  • ML-34, superseded. Its aim, a canonical JPEG XL that a browser shows with HDR brightness, is dropped (D4, D5). Its open decisions are answered here:
    • Its D1, how the light above white is kept: by none of its three forms, but as linear half-float, the "float/linear form" its §3 named (D1).
    • Its D3, the store or the front only: both (D1).
    • Its D4, whether the models read the light above white: no, an sRGB read-back (D2), as its research recommended.
    • Its D2, whether the models' input stays exactly ML-29's: no. Every photo is re-canonicalized and the classifier refitted (D13). How close the input stays is D6's choice.
    Its measurements carry over. Experiment A: in the median gain-map photo, 1.5% of bean pixels lie above the EETF's knee and 0.3% above white, the iPhones most.
  • The pinned encoder can write D1's form, checked on 2026-11-23 on a synthetic ramp. imagecodecs 2026.10.10's jpegxl_encode, given float16 samples and primaries=BT2100, writes a lossy (XYB) 16-bit float codestream: BT2100 primaries, the linear transfer, D65. Values above 1.0 survive (4.0 in, 4.02 out), and it decodes back to float16. The repo's own strip encoder, jxl_encode.encode_rec2020, which keeps the front's memory down (ML-19 D52), writes uint16 only (JXL_TYPE_UINT16) and needs a float16 form. The intensity target was not checked (D8).
  • How many bits the classifier's input needs, researched 2026-11-23 at the owner's request (research_bits_classifier.md): 8-, 10-, 12-bit and float16 sRGB against float32, each rounded once at the crop with the resizes after it in float. Going above 8 bits gives no measurable gain.
    • DINOv3's feature moves by a median 1 − cosine of 3.5e-7 at 8 bits, 2.2e-8 at 10, 1.5e-9 at 12 and 3.8e-9 at float16, on 1,600 test patches of 266 photos. For scale: the fp16 cast serving applies, 3.8e-8; the store's own lossy coding, 1.1e-5; a 1-pixel shift of the patch, 6.6e-4. From 10 bits up the input is as close to float as serving's own cast.
    • The live head (exp290) gives the same answer in every arm on all 1,600 patches and 266 photos: test macro-F1 0.9554 and the same F1 in all 10 classes.
    • Head refitted on seeds 42/123/7, 8-bit against float16: macro-F1 −0.0007, +0.0000, −0.0006, mean −0.0004, photo-bootstrap 95% CI [−0.0015, +0.0006], inside D13's proposed bar; the lowest class, Colombia, −0.0024. Unaugmented heads (the 8 views would have taken about 14 hours); 10 and 12 bits not refitted.
    • Where the rounding sits matters more than how many bits: today's path rounds three times (crop, 448 px patch, 224 px input), moves the feature 130× more than one 8-bit rounding at the crop, and is the only bit-depth arm that changes answers (4 patches, 2 photos; macro-F1 0.9559). The 8-bit effect is largest on boxes under 300 px, which are upsampled, but stays below a 1-pixel shift.
    • At the pixel, 8-bit rounding (0.29 levels RMS) is below the store's coding error (1.03) but not below the noise in the flattest bean regions (0.15); the downscale to the model's input averages it away (0.033 against the coding's 0.20).
    So D3 is a correctness and parity choice for the classifier, not an accuracy one; float16 crops and patches (D10 (b)) are more than enough, and a 10- or 12-bit store would also lose nothing if R8 or R9 binds. All arms use today's canonical photo, not D1's.
  • How many bits the segmenter's input needs, researched 2026-11-23/24 the same way (research_bits_segmenter.md, numbers in bits_seg_report.txt): the whole photo rounded once where load_canonical rounds today, the resize to the encoder's 1,024 px in float. The live segmenter is the XL0 encoder with the xl0_v7r_s123 decoder: the owner's request names XL1, which is downloaded but not used (params.yaml, the decoder's card). Going above 8 bits gives no measurable gain.
    • The encoder's embedding moves by a median 1 − cosine of 7.9e-7 at 8 bits, 3.5e-8 at 10, 2.0e-9 at 12 and 3.7e-9 at float16, on 625 photos. For scale: an fp16 cast of the input, 3.2e-8; a second q95 JPEG XL generation, 8.6e-6; a 1-pixel shift, 3.8e-4. From 10 bits up the input is as close to float as an fp16 cast.
    • Masks against the float32 arm's, 360 test photos: median IoU 0.99999 at 8 bits (lowest 0.998 on a served mask) and 1.00000 from 10 bits up; no mask became empty and no photo changed between mask and fallback in any arm. Crop boxes moved on 9 of 237 positives at 8 bits (at most 4 px), on none from 10 bits up except one at float16 (1 px).
    • Against the pass17 labels (validation, 177 positives and 88 negatives, seg_finetune's own measures): mean IoU 0.96990 in float32, float16, 12 bits and today's path, 0.96989 at 10 bits and 0.96992 at 8 bits; paired change 8-bit against float32 +1.6e-5, photo-bootstrap 95% CI [−6.9e-6, +5.1e-5]. Every negative stays empty in every arm. The test split has no labels (the owner reviews it blind).
    • As for the classifier, where the rounding sits matters more than how many bits: today's path rounds twice (the photo, then the 1,024 px input) and moves the embedding 30× more than one 8-bit rounding, and its masks most (test: lowest IoU 0.990 on a served mask, boxes moved on 30 of 237 positives; validation: one box by 114 px). It still scores the same against the labels.
    Reduced: 360 of 472 test photos within the time budget; no fine-tune, so augmentation's 8-bit steps (R5) are untested here. All arms use today's canonical photo, not D1's.

3. Before and after

Now

  • The canonical photo is Rec.2020 with the BT.709 curve, 16-bit integer, standard range: light above white is tone-mapped away when the photo is canonicalized.
  • Every reader rounds it to 8-bit sRGB first. Crops are stored at 8 bits, and patches are augmented and resized at 8 bits.
  • The preview is a JPEG of that 8-bit sRGB. OPS-25's trial adds three wide-gamut previews and format tabs.
  • LittleCMS does every colour step, both ways.

After

  • The canonical photo is linear Rec.2020 in 16-bit half-float, 1.0 = reference white, lossy JPEG XL. Light above white is kept.
  • One float32 read-back (matrix, tone map or clip, sRGB curve) feeds both models, from the decode to the model's input with no 8-bit step.
  • The preview is that read-back's picture: the visitor sees what the models read. No tabs, two parts.
  • LittleCMS still converts originals into Rec.2020. The read-back's maths is D9's choice.

4. What does not change

  • The models read standard-range sRGB. ML-19 D2; the evidence in §2 supports keeping it. Neither model's architecture or weights change; the classifier's head and the OOD probe are refitted on the new input.
  • One canonical photo per photo, JPEG XL, Rec.2020 primaries, made at the front (ADR 0033). Its encoding changes; its role does not.
  • What is read from an original. Colour declarations are honoured or refused (ADR 0040), and gain maps are applied by gain_map.py (ADR 0041). Only the tone map after them moves (D6).
  • The two settings. The store's and the front's quality, effort and caps stay (ADR 0036). The front's colour path is D12's.
  • The format list, the one streamed request, the masks' geometry (ADR 0034, ADR 0023).

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The models' input moves more than the change of form explains. Unmeasured. Before any retrain, the new read-back is compared with today's 8-bit input on every dataset photo: mean and largest level difference on 32×32 block means (ML-29 D27's measure), per camera, with the gain-map photos apart. Photos with nothing above white should differ only by the lossy coding and the lost rounding. The gain-map photos differ by D6's choice.
R2Lossy JPEG XL keeps less detail from linear half-float input than from today's BT.709 integers. libjxl turns either input into XYB before it quantizes, so the loss should be alike. But its perceptual quantization reads the intensity target (D8), and linear input puts few code values in the shadows. Measured in R1's comparison, and on the file size at both settings. Research, 2026-11-23 (D8's note): on 20 dataset photos at intensity target 255, linear half-float reads back with the same block-mean error as today's uint16 BT.709 encode (1.00×). At 203 the error is 1.09–1.10× and the file 4.5–6% smaller, still at least 8× inside D27.
R3The front runs out of time or memory. The front's integer path writes uint16 Rec.2020 and cannot write linear half-float as it is (D12). Gain maps already take it to 392–487 MB and 12–15 s on the largest photos, against its 600M (ML-29 R6). The tone map's peak pass leaves the front for the read-back. Measured on ML-29 R6's photos before the release. Research, 2026-11-23 (D12's note, on a loaded machine): the float path with a chunked half-float encoder takes 4.4–7.1 s and 312–396 MB on the largest gain-map photos (today 11.7–15.5 s), and at worst 10.6 s and 433 MB on any photo. Handing libjxl the whole picture instead peaks at 597 MB.
R4The inference host's read-back is slower or larger. A 15 MP photo in float32 is 180 MB per copy, against today's uint16 (90 MB) plus uint8 (45 MB). The host has about 3.6 GB beside the worker (OPS-10). The tone map needs the whole picture's peak before any strip is written. Measured against ML-21's decode time.
R5Float resizes and augmentations do not match today's 8-bit ones. PIL's bilinear filter widens on a downscale. A different float resizer changes what the segmenter and DINOv3 see (D11). Corrected 2026-11-23 by D11's research: no transform in params.yaml refuses float input, so nothing fails loudly. Three, ImageCompression, HueSaturationValue and Downscale, accept it but round it to 8 bits inside (albucore's @uint8_io), against D3. CoarseDropout's fill: [124, 116, 104] is taken in the picture's units and fills float holes with 124.0; on [0, 1] it is [0.486, 0.455, 0.408]. Off the augmentations, _to_chw_normalised's /255 and the bean-pitch grey's astype(uint8) assume 8-bit input. The plan changes each, with a test that a float pipeline's output is not on the 8-bit grid.
R6An old canonical photo meets a new host, or the reverse. The header check is the gate. The front and the host ship in one release, and the example photos are re-made in it. A canonical photo sent by a tool from before the release is refused, naming its encoding.
R78-bit steps survive off the main path. The bean-pitch estimate reads 8-bit grey (infer.py, dataset.py). The review pages, review_masks and make_example_photos read load_canonical. The plan lists every caller. A tool that only displays may round to 8 bits at its own end; anything that feeds a model may not (D3).
R8The new crops do not fit on this machine's disk. Research, 2026-11-23 (D10's note): /workspace has 62 GB free, and DVC keeps each crop set three times (cache, session folders, pool) because ext4 has no reflink. The new crops need about +85 GB under D10 (b) and +150 GB under (a). Mitigation: push, then clear the old crops from the local cache (ADR 0028, docs/agents/dvc.md) before the re-crop. The remote's free space is not yet checked.
R9Float patches and photos do not fit in memory. Research, 2026-11-23: 448 px patches in float16 take 7.23 GB for the training split against 3.61 GB in uint8, a peak of about 8.1 GB against about 7 GB available (D10's note). The segmenter's training set takes 2.6 GB in uint8, 5.1 GB in float16 and 10.3 GB in float32 (D11's note). The plan has to stream or memory-map them, or hold float16 where the fit allows it.

6. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1How is the canonical photo stored? — 2026-11-23, the owner's request: lossy JPEG XL, linear Rec.2020 primaries, D65, 16-bit half-float, 1.0 = reference (diffuse) white, light above white kept. In the store and on the front alike. Amends ADR 0033 and ML-19 D7, ADR 0040's PQ and HLG consequence and ADR 0041's "tone-mapped back".
D2How are both models' inputs made? — 2026-11-23, the owner's request: linear Rec.2020 (fp16) → 3×3 matrix → tone-map or clip to [0, 1] → sRGB curve. One read-back for DINOv3 and EfficientViT-SAM, in training and in serving.
D3The precision on the models' path? — 2026-11-23, the owner's request: no 8-bit step from the decoded 16 bits to the float32 model input (float16 where a model takes it). Every model graph takes float32 at its input today (§2), so the input is float32. Research, 2026-11-23: for the classifier, no measurable gain over 8 bits rounded once at the crop; from 10 bits up indistinguishable from float. The same for the segmenter, rounding once at the photo (§2).
D4What is the preview? — 2026-11-23, the owner's request: the same picture as the models' input, so the visitor sees what the model reads (OPS-7 D8's promise, back). Wide gamut is not adopted: OPS-25's trial, its tabs and its third part go, and preview_formats.py with them. Read here as today's file: an untagged sRGB JPEG made from the read-back's float sRGB, rounded to 8 bits only in that display file.
D5ML-34 and ML-29? — 2026-11-23, the owner's request: ML-34 is superseded by this ticket and closed. Its HDR-brightness preview is dropped, its open decisions answered in §2. This ticket continues ML-29 (closed).
D6Light above white at the read-back: tone-map or clip? (a) D33's EETF at the picture's own peak, applied after the matrix, so the gain-map photos read close to today (b) clip at 1.0: simplest; the gain-map photos' highlights clip, 0.3% of bean pixels in the median one (c) a fixed roll-off, the same for every picture Open. Recommended (a): only it keeps today's input for the 1,684 gain-map photos, so the retrain measures the new pipeline rather than a new look. Photos with nothing above white read the same under all three. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d6_tone_map_or_clip.md), recommends (a) with one correction: the EETF's gain comes from max(R, G, B) of the linear Rec.2020 values, as today. It scales a pixel's three channels by one number, so it commutes with the matrix and D2's order stays. Then all 23 gain-map photos measured read within D27 of today (median 0.05 of a level on bean blocks, worst photo 0.12). The gain taken from the sRGB max moves the median photo 0.6 and the worst 5.8; (b) 0.5 and 8.0; (c) also changes photos with nothing above white. Crops must use the whole photo's peak. The peak pass costs 0.55–0.62 s a 12 MP photo, under 0.1 s sampled.
D7Colours outside Rec.2020 at canonicalization (negative values, which half-float can hold)? (a) clipped at 0 as today; values above 1.0 kept (b) kept, clipped only by the read-back Open. Recommended (a): the store's numbers stay inside the gamut it names, and nothing in the dataset needs more. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d7_out_of_gamut.md), recommends (b), against this ticket's (a). Clipping before the encode does not keep the decoded file in gamut: libjxl's float decoder does not clamp, and photos clipped at 0 still decode with up to 5.8% of pixels below 0, so the read-back clips anyway. Out of gamut, (a) shifts hue (for example +38 levels of red) where (b) matches the original converted straight to sRGB. The dataset reads the same either way: Display P3 sources reach −0.0012 at most, and RAW development already clips. The tone map is unaffected.
D8The JPEG XL intensity target: how many cd/m² is 1.0? (a) 203, BT.2408's reference white, the one D33 already gives PQ (b) libjxl's default, 255 Open. Recommended (a), with its effect on lossy quality measured in R2. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d8_intensity_target.md), agrees: for a linear file the target is the luminance of (1, 1, 1), so D1's reference white is 203. libjxl scales light by target / 255 before XYB, which acts like about 1 quality point lower (R2). Decoded values do not depend on it. imagecodecs cannot set it; the ctypes encoder sets it with one field, and the header reader needs a few lines to read it.
D9Who does the read-back's maths? (a) numpy in float32: the matrix from the two sets of primaries, the sRGB curve in closed form, both tested against LittleCMS (b) LittleCMS's float transform, as ML-19 D31 has it Open. Recommended (a): from linear to linear it is a matrix only, so it is exact and easy to run strip by strip and threaded. Amends ADR 0033's "one library does all colour". Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d9_readback_maths.md), agrees, and notes that (a) reverses ML-19 D31. On 20 photos, LittleCMS is up to 0.15 of an 8-bit level off a float64 reference (its profiles' s15.16 values), numpy 0.0002. numpy takes 0.89 s at 15 MP against LittleCMS's 1.99 s (2.76 s with the two transforms D6 needs) and today's 1.85 s. Pin numpy exactly, and test against independent references.
D10How are crops and patches kept, now that they cannot be 8-bit? (a) crops as lossless 16-bit integer JPEG XL of the read-back's sRGB values; patches in memory as float16 (b) crops as lossless half-float JPEG XL (c) no crop store: crops cut from the read-back when the head is fitted Open. Recommended (a): steps of 1/65,535, far finer than anything the models resolve, and the crop store keeps its role. Amends ML-19 D32. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d10_crop_and_patch_storage.md), recommends (b), against this ticket's (a): both are bit-exact, and half-float crops are 37% smaller (the pool: 13.2 GB today, 57.1 GB under (a), 35.8 GB under (b)). DINOv3's embedding moves by 1 − cosine 3e-7 in float16, 1.2e-7 in uint16 and 2.2e-5 in uint8: (a)'s extra bits are lost one step later, where patches are float16 and DINOv3 serves in fp16. (c) is the fallback if disk binds (R8). Either way the patches need more memory than the fit has (R9).
D11The resizer for float input (sam_resize, eval_resize, the patch store)? (a) PIL's bilinear in float mode ("F"), channel by channel: today's filter (b) OpenCV (c) torch's antialiased interpolate Open. Recommended (a): the same filter on more bits, checked against today's at parity. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d11_float_resize_and_augment.md), agrees: on 13 photos the float path is within 2.02 levels of today's 8-bit output (mean 0.26–0.31), and about 2× slower. (b) is a different filter, up to 90 levels off. (c) matches PIL but cannot run on the serving hosts, so it becomes the test oracle. The augmentations' findings are in R5.
D12The front's colour path (ADR 0036's integer path writes uint16 Rec.2020)? (a) the store's float path for every upload (b) a faster path found by measurement, held within a stated tolerance of (a) Open. To be measured first (R3). Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d12_front_colour_path.md), recommends (a), with a chunked JXL_TYPE_FLOAT16 encoder (the same bytes as a whole-picture encode), the gain-map light kept in float16, and no peak pass. Gain-map photos get faster (11.7 → 4.4 s, 15.5 → 7.1 s), plain photos about 2.4 s slower, worst 10.6 s and 433 MB against 40 s and 600M (R3). Files grow 4.5–9% on gain-map photos. (b) exists if needed: per-channel tables, 7–10× faster and within 0.14 of a level.
D13What does the retrain have to show, fixed before the runs? (a) no regression: the classifier refitted on seeds 42, 123 and 7, paired with today's (ADR 0002), the overall test metric and no class's F1 dropping beyond the seed range (b) an improvement beyond the seed range, as ML-29 D16 Open. Recommended (a): the aim is a correct pipeline with nothing lost, not a gain in accuracy, which §2's evidence does not promise. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d13_adoption_bar.md), agrees, and rewords (a) because the seed range mostly measures which photos land in test, and the per-class rule as written misfires on easy and hard classes alike. Proposed bar: the mean paired change in test macro-F1 over the three seeds, with its 95% photo-bootstrap lower bound above −0.010; no class's mean paired F1 change below −0.02; the OOD probe still catching every test negative and refusing at most 4 of the 295 bean photos; before the retrain, a median mask IoU of at least 0.99 per camera against today's. (b) needs about +0.019, more than any change in the record. The bootstrap is new code.
D14The segmenter, and ML-33's fine-tune? (a) xl0_v7r_s123 kept and run on the new input; ML-33 fine-tunes after this ticket, on the new canonical photos (b) ML-33 first, on today's input, then this ticket Open. Recommended (a): one fine-tune on the input it will serve, not two. Research, 2026-11-23 (research_d14_segmenter_and_ml33.md), recommends (b), against this ticket's (a): on 20 test photos, the live segmenter's masks from a simulated ML-37 input have a median IoU of 0.9999 against today's (lowest 0.989), mostly from the float resize. Nothing in a fine-tune is keyed to the canonical photo's bytes (no embedding cache, labels keyed to the original), so ML-33 on today's input need not be redone; its stage is frozen afterwards. Choose (a) if D3 is read strictly to cover the fine-tune's augmentation.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • D6–D14 decided; the ADRs they amend (0033, 0036, 0040, 0041) amended, and a new ADR for the linear canonical photo and its read-back.
  • The canonical photo written as D1 says by both settings, the store's and the front's, and refused in any other form by the host. Shown by jxlinfo (linear transfer, Rec.2100 primaries, 16-bit float, lossy) and by tests.
  • The read-back as D2 says, with no 8-bit step on any path to either model (D3). Every caller of load_canonical and every uint8 assumption on the models' path is listed and changed (R7).
  • R1's comparison on every dataset photo, per camera, before any retrain; R2's file sizes at both settings.
  • The front's and the host's time and memory on ML-29 R6's photos (R3, R4).
  • The store re-canonicalized and the pool re-cropped through their DVC stages, and pushed to the DVC remote.
  • The classifier's head and the OOD probe refitted, and the paired three-seed comparison judged against D13's bar. The owner's decision.
  • The preview is the models' input picture (D4). OPS-25's tabs, third part and preview_formats.py removed. GLOSSARY.md's "Preview" and "Canonicalization" say so.
  • Adopted: one release of store, front, host and models, the examples re-made, verified on the live site. Not adopted: the reasons recorded here and in .out-of-scope/.

Out of scope

  • A preview with wide gamut or HDR brightness (OPS-25, ML-34). The canonical photo keeps the light, so a later ticket can make one when browsers draw it.
  • Light above white, PU21 or any other HDR encoding as the models' input (ML-34 D4, experiment B).
  • Changes to what is read from an original: colour declarations, gain-map forms, second images (ML-29).
  • New models or a change of backbone; capture metadata (ML-30).
