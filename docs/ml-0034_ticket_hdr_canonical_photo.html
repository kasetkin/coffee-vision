1. Summary

A photo with a gain map, or an HDR main image, holds light above SDR white. Canonicalization applies the map and tone-maps that light back into standard range (ADR 0041, ML-29 D33), so the canonical photo, and any preview made from it, is never brighter than white. This ticket would keep that light in the canonical photo, as a PQ or HLG transfer or a JPEG XL gain map, and give the models a read-back that tone-maps it exactly as canonicalization does today, so their input does not change.

2. Background

  • The canonical photo today. Rec.2020, BT.709 transfer, 16-bit lossy JPEG XL, standard range; its 16 bits are finer steps, not headroom (ADR 0033, ADR 0041).
  • How much it concerns. 1,687 of the dataset's 2,772 photos carry a gain map, 1,684 of them applied (ADR 0041, ML-29 P8).
  • The browsers. Safari shows HDR images in <img> from Safari 26; Chrome shows gain-map JPEG and AVIF as HDR by a reading of its source, not by a test (research_browser_hdr_preview.md). Whether either shows an HDR JPEG XL is not researched.
  • The front's cost. Applying a map already takes the front to 392–487 MB and 12–15 s for the largest photos, against its 600 MB (ADR 0041, ML-29 R6).

3. Before and after

Now

  • The canonical photo is standard range; light above white is tone-mapped away at canonicalization.
  • No preview is brighter than SDR white.

After

  • The canonical photo keeps the light above white.
  • The models' read-back tone-maps it to the same 8-bit sRGB as today.
  • On an HDR screen and browser, the preview's highlights go above white.

4. What does not change

  • What the models read, if D2 holds: the 8-bit sRGB of ML-29's canonical photo.
  • One canonical photo per photo (ADR 0033): kept in one file, not two.

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The read-back's tone map differs from today's, so the models' input changes. Unmeasured. Compare the read-back with today's canonical photos on the dataset before anything else; a difference means a re-canonicalization and a retrain.
R2The front runs out of memory or time. It is already near its 600 MB for the largest gain-map photos (ADR 0041). Measured as ML-29 R6 was.
R3The browsers do not show the chosen HDR form. Unresearched for JPEG XL. Tested on the Pixel 9 Pro and an iPhone before the canonical form is chosen.

6. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1How does the canonical photo keep the light above white? (a) a PQ or HLG transfer (b) a JPEG XL gain map over today's standard-range image (c) a separate HDR preview rendition made at the front, the canonical photo unchanged Open.
D2Must the models' input stay ML-29's exactly? (a) yes, a read-back that reproduces D33's tone map (b) no, re-canonicalize and retrain Open.
D3Does the store change too, or only the front? (a) both (b) the front only (a further departure in ADR 0036) Open.

7. Scope and acceptance

  • D1–D3 decided, and the ADRs they amend (0033, 0036, 0041) amended.
  • The models' read-back measured against today's canonical photos on the dataset (R1).
  • The front's memory and time measured on the largest gain-map photos (R2).
  • The owner has seen the bright preview on a Pixel 9 Pro and an iPhone.

Out of scope

  • The preview's wide gamut and its format: OPS-25.
  • Showing the visitor's own file as the preview (OPS-25 D3 (b), not taken).
