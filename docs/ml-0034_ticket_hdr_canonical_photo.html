1. Summary

A photo with a gain map, or an HDR main image, holds light above SDR white. Canonicalization applies the map and tone-maps that light back into standard range (ADR 0041, ML-29 D33), so the canonical photo, and any preview made from it, is never brighter than white. This ticket would keep that light in the canonical photo, as a PQ or HLG transfer or a JPEG XL gain map, and give the models a read-back that tone-maps it exactly as canonicalization does today, so their input does not change.

2. Background

  • The canonical photo today. Rec.2020, BT.709 transfer, 16-bit lossy JPEG XL, standard range; its 16 bits are finer steps, not headroom (ADR 0033, ADR 0041).
  • How much it concerns. 1,687 of the dataset's 2,772 photos carry a gain map, 1,684 of them applied (ADR 0041, ML-29 P8).
  • The browsers. Safari shows HDR images in <img> from Safari 26; Chrome shows gain-map JPEG and AVIF as HDR by a reading of its source, not by a test (research_browser_hdr_preview.md). Whether either shows an HDR JPEG XL is not researched.
  • The front's cost. Applying a map already takes the front to 392–487 MB and 12–15 s for the largest photos, against its 600 MB (ADR 0041, ML-29 R6).
  • The storage forms the pinned libjxl can write. imagecodecs 2026.10.10 bundles libjxl 0.12.0: PQ, HLG and float linear encode and decode (checked), with no intensity target set, so 10,000 cd/m² for PQ. A jhgm gain map has no ready-made writer in the wheel (JxlGainMap*Bundle not exported); it would be serialised by our own code and added with JxlEncoderAddBox, and libjxl does not apply it on decode (research_hdr_pu21.md §4).

3. Research: HDR light for the models, and PU21 (2026-11-22)

The owner asked whether the models could gain from the HDR light too, through the PU21 curve, and where the curve would sit. Research note: analysis/ml34/research_hdr_pu21.md (primary sources, §1–5; the experiments, §6). A finding, not a decision.

  • The literature. HDR or RAW input helped detectors under extreme light (≈18-stop scenes, exposures 4–10× off, the dark) and when the network or a learned input stage was trained on the new encoding. Under ordinary light it was within about 1 mAP of SDR; linear light into an sRGB-pretrained network was clearly worse (−5 to −19 AP). No study tests a frozen ViT with a linear probe, our case; the nearest (Chubarau et al. 2024) found sRGB-pretrained networks without fine-tuning good on PU21-encoded SDR and weak on PU21-encoded HDR.
  • PU21 (Mantiuk & Azimi 2021, reference code gfxdisp/pu21): one closed-form curve, V = p7·(((p1 + p2·Yp4)/(1 + p3·Yp4))p5 − p6), on absolute luminance Y from 0.005 to 10,000 cd/m²; the authors recommend the banding_glare parameters only. 100 cd/m² maps to 256, 10,000 to 595. It is a perceptually uniform encoding for metrics and networks, not a transfer function any codec, browser or screen knows. On an SDR photo it is not sRGB: midtones sit 20–36 levels higher.
  • Where it would sit. Only at the models' read-back, never in the store or the preview: canonical photo → linear light (1.0 = SDR white) → linear sRGB primaries, negatives clipped → × 100 cd/m² → PU21 per channel → divided by PU21 of the chosen top → the crop's fill → ImageNet normalisation as today. It replaces D33's tone map and the sRGB curve in load_canonical for the models only. So it needs the store to keep the light above white (D1 (a) or (b), or a float/linear form), and it changes every photo's input, SDR photos included: it is D2 (b), both models retrained, never D2 (a). For the classifier only bean pixels count: outside the mask the crop is grey.
  • Experiment A: is there HDR light on the beans? 206 gain-map photos with a segmenter mask, light read before the tone map (analysis/ml34/a_light_above_white.py). The median photo has 1.5% of its bean pixels above the EETF's knee (changed by the tone map) and 0.3% above white; 72 photos have over 5% above the knee, 33 over 5% above white; the iPhones most (9–29% above the knee in each model's median photo), the Pixel 9 Pro least (0.5%). The knee is well below white (0.60 at Apple's median 2.7× headroom).
  • Experiment B: the frozen head on PU21 crops (analysis/ml34/b_pu21_probe.py; unaugmented heads only, C = 0.1, seeds 42/123/7, the same patches; a screen, not a recorded run). Paired against today's crops on test macro-F1, mean of three seeds, lower at every seed: PU21 in place of sRGB −0.004; PU21 with 2 stops of headroom reserved −0.008; the same with the true HDR light in that headroom −0.004. Against a control with the same originals, boxes and curve, tone-mapped as the store does, the HDR light alone gives +0.002 on test and +0.004 on val (by seed: test +0.004, +0.001, +0.002), inside the seed range and nothing at one seed; the rest of the HDR arm's edge over the store's crops was the lossless original against the store's lossy JPEG XL (+0.0015) (§6 of the note). Chubarau et al.'s full-range scaling (PU21 of 10,000 cd/m² = the top), tried after the papers' review, does no better: −0.004 on test against today's crops, −0.0003 against 4× white.
  • The papers that report PU21 helping sRGB-pretrained networks (X2HDR, Chubarau et al. 2024, LumaGuide, FDIM; research_pu21_papers.md): HDR generation and quality metrics, not classification with a frozen backbone. Their gain is over linear HDR input on true HDR content, or comes from training on PU input; our baseline is sRGB of a tone-mapped photo and our crops are almost all standard range. Where a paper feeds a frozen network both, the SDR input wins, as it did here.
  • So: the bright preview (this ticket's aim) needs no PU21. HDR input for the models is a separate question (D4): on this dataset the curve costs more than the light gives, under an unaugmented head.

4. Before and after

Now

  • The canonical photo is standard range; light above white is tone-mapped away at canonicalization.
  • No preview is brighter than SDR white.

After

  • The canonical photo keeps the light above white.
  • The models' read-back tone-maps it to the same 8-bit sRGB as today.
  • On an HDR screen and browser, the preview's highlights go above white.

5. What does not change

  • What the models read, if D2 holds: the 8-bit sRGB of ML-29's canonical photo.
  • One canonical photo per photo (ADR 0033): kept in one file, not two.

6. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1The read-back's tone map differs from today's, so the models' input changes. Unmeasured. Compare the read-back with today's canonical photos on the dataset before anything else; a difference means a re-canonicalization and a retrain. Experiment A says where it would show: in the median gain-map photo 1.5% of bean pixels lie above the EETF's knee, up to 61%, so the read-back must reproduce the EETF at the whole picture's peak (D33), not a clip, and a store that drops light (a PQ intensity target, an 8-bit map) moves those pixels.
R2The front runs out of memory or time. It is already near its 600 MB for the largest gain-map photos (ADR 0041). Measured as ML-29 R6 was.
R3The browsers do not show the chosen HDR form. Unresearched for JPEG XL. Tested on the Pixel 9 Pro and an iPhone before the canonical form is chosen.
R4An HDR input to the models (D4 (b)) lowers accuracy. Measured on unaugmented heads (§3, experiment B; both scalings, 4× white and full range): PU21 −0.004 to −0.008 on test against today's sRGB crops, the HDR light alone giving back +0.002. Only a recorded, augmented, paired three-seed run under ADR 0002's bar may change the models' input.

7. Decisions

IDQuestionOptionsOwner decision
D1How does the canonical photo keep the light above white? (a) a PQ or HLG transfer (b) a JPEG XL gain map over today's standard-range image (c) a separate HDR preview rendition made at the front, the canonical photo unchanged Open.
D2Must the models' input stay ML-29's exactly? (a) yes, a read-back that reproduces D33's tone map (b) no, re-canonicalize and retrain Open.
D3Does the store change too, or only the front? (a) both (b) the front only (a further departure in ADR 0036) Open.
D4Do the models read the light above white too? (a) no: the light is for the preview; the models' read-back tone-maps it as today (D2 (a)) (b) yes: a PU21 read-back (§3: 1.0 = 100 cd/m², per channel on linear sRGB primaries, a top of N× white), D2 (b), adopted only by a recorded run that beats today's under ADR 0002 Open. Research recommends (a): experiment B found no gain on this dataset (§3).

8. Scope and acceptance

  • D1–D4 decided, and the ADRs they amend (0033, 0036, 0041) amended.
  • The models' read-back measured against today's canonical photos on the dataset (R1).
  • The front's memory and time measured on the largest gain-map photos (R2).
  • The owner has seen the bright preview on a Pixel 9 Pro and an iPhone.

Out of scope

  • The preview's wide gamut and its format: OPS-25.
  • Showing the visitor's own file as the preview (OPS-25 D3 (b), not taken).
