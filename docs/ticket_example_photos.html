Ticket OPS-16 · coffee-vision · the page, the front and the deploy

The page offers five example photos, and the front takes photos up to 10 MB

A visitor without a photo of coffee beans cannot try the service today. This ticket adds five example photos to the page, each sent through the same upload as a visitor's own. The photos leave every training and evaluation set, in ML-16's data change, and both tickets ship in one release. The same release raises the front's upload cap from 5 MB to 10 MB. Written 2026-11-03 from a grilling session with the owner; every decision taken, not planned, not implemented.

1. Summary

Under "Choose Photo" the page gets a row of five example photos (D1, D10): four bean photos, each from a different country and setting, and a cappuccino that the OOD guard should refuse. A click fetches the example's file from the front and uploads it to /analyze, as if the visitor had chosen it (D9). The files are lossy JPEG XL re-encodes at full resolution, committed in webapp/static/examples/ with a small WebP thumbnail each (D2, D3, D7). Their originals leave the dataset for a private folder no pipeline reads, so no model the visitor's answer comes from has seen them (D5, ADR 0032). Every deploy's --verify checks each example's expected answer (D11).

The front's upload cap goes from 5 MB to 10 MB, with longer timeouts to match (D8).

The work splits into: the files and their check against the originals; the page; the front (the cap, the examples' route); the deploy check. The dataset change belongs to ML-16 (its D3).

  • The cap. The front refuses a photo over 5 MB (OPS-15 D15, ADR 0031) in four places: the front's nginx (client_max_body_size 5184k and its 413), check_service.py's MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES, index.html's MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES, and the docs for agents (llms.txt, openapi.json). The inference host allows 20 MB already (its nginx, app.py).
  • The link. Photos cross to yappy at about 2.1 Mbit/s (measured 2026-11-02): 10 MB takes about 40 s. The check service gives the whole upload 60 s (INFERENCE_TIMEOUT_S), and the front's nginx waits 130 s (proxy_read_timeout). One photo at a time crosses (the front's gate).
  • The check service's memory. It reads the whole upload into memory (check_service.py, self.rfile.read(length)). It peaked at 28 MB live and 40 MB in the plan's test with a 5 MB photo, under MemoryMax=100M, on a proxy with about 200 MB available (OPS-15 R1).
  • The front's static files. nginx serves a fixed list of paths and answers 404 to any other; release_manifest.py --front lists the files a front release holds.
  • JPEG XL in browsers. Safari shows it; Firefox reportedly since 158 (October 2026); Chrome only behind a flag as of Chrome 145; Edge not at all. The page never has to show one: the picker shows WebP thumbnails (D7), and after a click the server's own JPEG thumbnail. The front's format check and the app's decoder already take JPEG XL.
  • Splits. The classifier's split shuffles each class's sorted pool by position (coffeecv/dataset.py, split_photos_by_class), so removing one photo redraws the whole class.

3. Before and after

Now

  • A visitor needs a photo of their own to try the service.
  • The five photos are in the private dataset, two of them in the live model's training split.
  • The front refuses a photo over 5 MB, a 50 MP phone photo (7 MB) among them.
  • --verify checks one photo's answer through the domain.

After

  • The page offers five example photos; a click runs the full service on one.
  • Their JPEG XL re-encodes are public in the repo; their originals are in dataset/example_photos/, in no training or evaluation set.
  • The front refuses a photo over 10 MB.
  • --verify checks the five examples' answers too.

4. What does not change

  • The API. /analyze takes the same form and answers the same way; an example is an ordinary upload (D9, ADR 0023).
  • The inference host. Its 20 MB limits already cover 10 MB.
  • The rest of the dataset stays private (ADR 0014); ADR 0032 publishes these five re-encodes only.
  • Re-encoding visitors' photos. The front still forwards a visitor's bytes unchanged (ML-15).

5. Risks

IDRiskEvidence and mitigation
R1A re-encode gets a different answer from its original. Lossy JPEG XL changes the pixels, and so does decoding through libjxl. Each example ships only once the model of ML-16's release gives it the original's answer and the owner accepts its mask (D3).
R2Slow examples. At 2.1 Mbit/s the Ethiopia example takes about 16 s to cross and the Brazil one about 13 s, before about 9 s of inference; the visitor also downloads it first. Accepted (D3); a lower quality for one photo is the fallback.
R3A 10 MB photo holds the gate for about 50 s, and fails on a slower link. 40 s to cross at 2.1 Mbit/s against today's 60 s upload timeout. The timeouts go to 120 s and 190 s; holding the gate is accepted (D8).
R4The check service runs out of memory on a 10 MB upload. 28-40 MB peak at 5 MB, under a 100 MB MemoryMax. Measured with a 10 MB upload before the release; MemoryMax rises only if the peak passes about 70 MB (D8).
R5Results before and after ML-16 are not paired. Removing the photos redraws the Brazil, Ethiopia, Vietnam and Indonesia splits; the segmenter test loses the Indonesia photo and the user_realworld holdout the cappuccino. ML-16's new photos redraw Ethiopia and Colombia anyway, so the change goes into the same data change (D6, ML-16 D3).
R6The Brazil photo shows a window onto the street. leak_check cannot judge what a photo shows. The owner looked and kept it: "this is public space" (D4).
R7A later model gets an example wrong. --verify fails (D11); the owner rolls back or swaps the example. A new example has to come from outside every set, or leave them before the next retrain (ADR 0032).

7. Scope and acceptance

  • The five originals in dataset/example_photos/ and out of every set (ML-16 D3).
  • Five lossy JPEG XL files and five WebP thumbnails in webapp/static/examples/; leak_check passes them.
  • On the model of ML-16's release, each example gets its original's answer, and the owner accepted each bean photo's mask at full resolution.
  • The front serves the examples (its nginx route, release_manifest.py --front).
  • The page's example row, the click, and the truth line after the answer (D9, D10).
  • The 10 MB cap everywhere the 5 MB one is: nginx, the check service, the page, llms.txt, openapi.json, webapp/README.md, docs/inference_path.html, ADR 0031, the tests; the timeouts raised (D8).
  • The check service's peak memory with a 10 MB upload, measured and written here.
  • --verify checks the five examples (D11).
  • Live with ML-16's release, verified on the domain.

Out of scope

  • Re-encoding visitors' photos at the front (ML-15).
  • Saved answers, or examples that work while the classifier is offline.
  • Listing the examples in llms.txt or openapi.json for agents.
